For years your phone sat on the nightstand, Sleep Cycle listened to you breathe, and the alarm caught you in a light patch of sleep. Then you got an Apple Watch, it started recording your nights too, and now you have two apps disagreeing about the same night (and one of them wants a renewal).

What Sleep Cycle does, and why people look elsewhere

Credit first. Sleep Cycle is one of the longest-running sleep apps on the App Store, with 4.7 stars from about 32,500 ratings in the US and a release as recent as November 12, 2026. Its listing describes tracking with "patented sound technology or accelerometer-based sleep detection, no wearables needed". You leave the phone by the bed and it listens, or you put it on the mattress and it feels you move. On top of that it has a Smart Alarm that wakes you in your lightest phase, snoring, coughing and sleep talking detection, a sound library and an AI sleep coach called Luma. Premium is a subscription; the US listing shows in-app purchases from $2.99 to $57.99 depending on the offer.

People who leave usually hit one of three walls:

  • They wear an Apple Watch now. The watch records heart rate, motion and sleep stages from your wrist, and Sleep Cycle's core tracking does not depend on it. A phone listening across the room and a watch on your arm will tell different stories.
  • The bed is not quiet. A partner, a snoring dog or a fan all sit in the same audio as you.
  • They want a one-time price, or simply fewer features and a clearer answer in the morning.

If you wear an Apple Watch to bed

Start with Apple's own Sleep app. It costs nothing and is probably already running. On iPhone, open the Health app, tap Get Started under Set Up Sleep, and follow the prompts for your sleep goal, bedtime and wake-up time, Sleep Focus and Track Sleep with Apple Watch. Each morning the watch's Sleep app shows Awake, REM, Core and Deep stages, and on watchOS 26 or later a Sleep Score from 0 to 100. Our step-by-step guide on how to track sleep on Apple Watch covers the setup and the common reasons a night comes up blank. The one thing it lacks next to Sleep Cycle: the sleep schedule alarm rings at the fixed Wake Up time you set, with no light-sleep window.

Nularo is our app, and it is the pick if what you liked about Sleep Cycle was opening it in the morning and getting a verdict. It is not a tracker. It reads the sleep your watch saves to Apple Health (read-only, along with steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and blood oxygen), so there is nothing to start at night. Each morning a notification arrives with the verdict, plus Sleep, Stress and Energy scores and a running sleep debt. A small creature lands in one of four moods, glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious, measured against your own 14-day baseline. A Bedtime Pledge sets the time you promise to be in bed, and a Weekly Report Card grades the week. The honest limits: no alarm, no snore recordings, no sleep sounds, and it works the morning after, not in real time. Without a watch the data is thin. It is iPhone only, a subscription with a hard paywall after onboarding, $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial or $9.99 a month.

AutoSleep is the most common answer from Apple Watch owners. It costs $9.99 once, and the listing says it has "no extra In App Purchases. No subscriptions" and no data upload. It can track on its own or turn Apple's Sleep app data into a full analysis: sleep rings, a sleep bank, sleep consistency, a readiness view, overnight blood oxygen and wrist temperature, plus a smart alarm built into the watch app. It needs an Apple Watch Series 4 or later. It is dense; if the screens look like a cockpit, our comparison of Apple's Sleep Score and AutoSleep explains which numbers matter.

Pillow is the closest feel to Sleep Cycle for watch wearers. It tracks automatically when you wear the watch to bed, or manually with the iPhone, and records snoring and sleep talking with on-device processing. The listing puts Smart Wake-up, sound recordings, heart rate analysis and unlimited history in Premium, which is $39.99 a year in the US. Its 4.4 stars come from about 96,000 ratings.

If you sleep without a watch

This is where Sleep Cycle is strongest, so the alternatives are closer to sideways moves.

SleepScore uses sonar from the iPhone on your nightstand to measure movement and breathing, with no contact. The listing says the method has been validated in 14 published papers against polysomnography (PSG, the lab sleep test). It writes sleep to Apple Health, has a smart alarm and weekly reports. Premium is $9.99 a month or $59.99 a year with a 7-day trial.

ShutEye leans on sound: a large library of sleep sounds, white noise and bedtime stories, plus a sleep tracker, snore tracking and a smart alarm. It saves sleep sessions to Apple Health. VIP is $59.99 a year per the listing.

Rise is different again. It is built around sleep debt and your energy curve through the day, using data from Apple Health, Apple Watch, Fitbit, Oura, and other phone trackers. Its listing names Sleep Cycle among them, so you can keep tracking with Sleep Cycle and use Rise for the debt number. It opens with a 7-day trial and then runs on yearly subscriptions ($69.99 a year in the US); our guide to Rise app alternatives lists cheaper ways to keep a debt number. If sleep debt is the number you want, our sleep debt calculator shows the math for free.

What you lose when you leave Sleep Cycle

  • The smart alarm, unless you pick AutoSleep, Pillow Premium, SleepScore, ShutEye or Rise. If you want an alarm that waits for a set amount of sleep instead, see how to set an Apple Watch alarm after 8 hours of sleep.
  • Snore and sleep-talk recordings. Watch-only trackers do not record audio. Pillow and ShutEye do.
  • Partner linking. Sleep Cycle can link two phones in one bed. Our guide to sleep tracking with a partner or friends covers what the others offer.
  • Your history. Export what you want to keep before you cancel.

Expect the numbers to change too. Every app here uses its own method on different sensors, so the same night can be 7h 10m in one app and 6h 40m in another. Pick one source and follow its trend. Frequently asked questions

Is there a free alternative to Sleep Cycle?

Yes. If you have an Apple Watch, Apple's built-in Sleep app tracks sleep stages and gives a 0 to 100 Sleep Score on watchOS 26 or later at no cost. Pillow, SleepScore and ShutEye are free to download with paid tiers, and Sleep Cycle itself has a free tier with its smart alarm.

Is AutoSleep better than Sleep Cycle?

They measure different things. AutoSleep reads your Apple Watch's heart rate and motion and costs $9.99 once, with no subscription and no data upload. Sleep Cycle listens with your iPhone's microphone or feels motion through the mattress, so it needs no watch and can detect snoring and sleep talking. Watch wearers usually prefer AutoSleep; people who sleep without a watch usually stay with Sleep Cycle.

Does Sleep Cycle work with Apple Watch?

Its US App Store listing shows an Apple Watch app (watchOS 9 or later), but the core tracking is built around the phone: the listing describes sound analysis or accelerometer detection with no wearables needed. If you want your watch's heart rate and sleep stages to drive the analysis, AutoSleep, Pillow or Apple's Sleep app fit better.

Which Sleep Cycle alternative has a smart alarm?

AutoSleep has a smart alarm built into its watch app, Pillow includes Smart Wake-up in Premium, and SleepScore, ShutEye and Rise all list a smart alarm. Apple's own sleep schedule alarm rings at the fixed Wake Up time you set.

Is Nularo a sleep tracker like Sleep Cycle?

No. Nularo does not record your night or ring an alarm. It reads the sleep your Apple Watch (or another app) saved to Apple Health and gives you a morning verdict, a Sleep score, sleep debt and a creature mood against your own 14-day baseline. It is iPhone only and costs $39.99 a year after a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month.
