Short answer

Finch is a free self-care pet app for iPhone and Android: you check off small goals, your bird gets energy and goes on an adventure, and you get mood tracking, reflections and breathing tools along the way. The core is free. Finch Plus is optional, listed by Finch at $9.99 a month or $69.99 a year. Everything is logged by hand: the App Store listing mentions no Apple Health, and there is no Apple Watch app. Nularo is the zero-logging opposite: a creature that reads your sleep and movement from Apple Health and judges you every morning.

How the Finch app works

You start with a baby bird (Finch calls it a "birb") and a few simple goals. Finch's own description of the loop has three steps. You do something for yourself, such as drinking water, stepping outside or making your bed. Each completed goal earns your bird energy or Rainbow Stones, the in-app currency. When the bird is fully energized, you send it on an adventure, and it comes back with a short story and sometimes a discovery.

Around that loop sits a toolbox. The App Store listing names a goal tracker, guided bullet journaling, breathing exercises, a mood tracker with trends, quizzes that track things like anxiety and body image appreciation, motivational quotes, an evening wind-down with breathing and next-day goal planning, and an Insights screen that combines your mood, journal tags, goals and quiz results. Finch's site adds soundscapes and a focus timer.

Then there is the game layer. You dress your bird, decorate its home and join seasonal events. November 2026's season is Hidden Hollow, where completing self-care tasks earns a weasel companion and woodland decorations. You can add friends and send each other "good vibes", which is about as social as it gets.

Missing a day is soft on purpose. Finch's help center says the streak counts on any day you open the app, Rainbow Stones can repair a broken streak, every 3 adventures earns a free streak repair (you can hold 2), and Pause Mode in Settings freezes everything while you are away.

Is Finch free? What Finch Plus adds and costs

Yes, and genuinely so. Finch's about page lists what stays free with no subscription: caring for your bird and its adventures, personal goals and reflections, good vibes to friends, and seasonal events. That is the whole core loop. There are no paywalled basics.

Finch Plus is extras: additional reflection prompts, more good vibe and goal emoji, expanded activities and timer options, and extra seasonal rewards and shop items. Finch also frames it as a way to support the team.

On price, Finch's help center lists Plus at $9.99 a month or $69.99 a year. The App Store shows nine Finch Plus purchases between $5.99 and $69.99 (offers, regional and promotional tiers), plus a $7.99 Guardian Program, so the number you see in the app may be lower. Two details worth knowing before you tap:

  • Two kinds of trial. Finch's help center describes a 3-day free Plus trial that ends on its own, and a 7-day free trial that turns into a paid subscription unless you cancel. Check which one you are being offered.
  • Free Plus if money is tight. Guardians are users who pay to fund Plus for others. Finch runs a monthly raffle for free Plus subscriptions; you enter from Settings under Become a Guardian.

Subscriptions run through Apple or Google Play, so you cancel in your App Store subscriptions, not inside Finch.

What Finch does well, and where it falls short

The good. The tone is kind without being clinical. Goals can be tiny ("get out of bed" counts), which is the point for people who are struggling. The free version is complete, not a demo. And the rating is not an accident: 4.9 across three quarters of a million ratings is rare in any category.

The trade-offs.

  • It is all manual. Finch does not read Apple Health, and its privacy label lists no health or fitness data. If your Apple Watch already logged a 40-minute walk and seven hours of sleep, Finch still needs you to tell it. There is no watch app either.
  • The game can take over. Shops, seasonal items and currencies keep it fun, but some long-time users find collecting starts to feel like a second to-do list. Our guide to Finch alternatives for adults groups the common complaints and the fix for each.
  • It is very cute. That is a feature for most users and a deal-breaker for some. If you want the warmth with a different look, the self-care tamagotchi apps roundup compares Finch with Quabble, Kinder World and other gentle pets.
  • English only, per the App Store listing.

Finch is a wellness app, not therapy. If you are using it to cope with something heavy, it can sit alongside real support, not replace it. Talk to a doctor or a professional if things feel like too much.

If you want the pet without the check-ins: Nularo

Nularo is our app, and it is built for the person who likes Finch's idea but not the logging. It is an iPhone app with a creature that reads Apple Health read-only: sleep and sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and blood oxygen. You never type anything in.

Each morning you get a verdict notification, and the creature lands in one of four moods (glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious) set against your own 14-day baseline. You also get Sleep, Stress and Energy scores, a running sleep debt, a Bedtime Pledge, and a weekly Creature Age: a rules-based, for-fun number from your sleep, bedtime consistency, steps, active minutes, resting heart rate and HRV, not a medical test. Friend mode shows a friend only your mood word and a good-day count, never your health numbers.

The honest limits. The tone is the opposite of Finch: it judges, it does not encourage or coach, and some days it will be rude about your five hours of sleep. It only sees what Apple Health records, so habits like journaling or calling a friend are invisible to it, and without an Apple Watch the data is thin. It is iPhone only, with a hard paywall after onboarding: $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month. There is no free tier like Finch's.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Finch app free?

Yes. Finch says its core features are free: caring for your bird, goals, reflections, good vibes to friends and seasonal events. Finch Plus is an optional subscription for extra prompts, activities, emoji and seasonal rewards. Finch's help center lists it at $9.99 a month or $69.99 a year.

How does the Finch app work?

You set small self-care goals and check them off. Each goal earns your bird energy or Rainbow Stones. Once the bird is fully energized it goes on an adventure and returns with a story. Mood checks, reflections, breathing and quizzes add more ways to earn.

Does Finch connect to Apple Health or Apple Watch?

Not as of November 2026. The App Store description does not mention Apple Health, the privacy label lists no health or fitness data, and the compatibility list covers iPhone, iPad and Apple Vision but not Apple Watch. Everything you track, you enter yourself.

Does Finch punish you for missing a day?

Not harshly. Finch's help center says your streak counts on any day you open the app, Rainbow Stones can repair a broken streak, you earn a free streak repair every 3 adventures (up to 2 saved), and Pause Mode in Settings freezes it during a break.

Is Nularo a Finch alternative?

For some people. It keeps the idea of a creature that reflects how you look after yourself, but it reads Apple Health instead of asking you to log, and it judges rather than encourages. It is iPhone only and a subscription ($39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month), with no free tier.
