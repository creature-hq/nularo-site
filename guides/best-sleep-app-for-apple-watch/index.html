You have an Apple Watch, you want it to tell you something useful about your nights, and the App Store search for "sleep" returns a wall of moons and sheep. Most of them want a subscription before they show you anything. Here is the short list, sorted by what each one is actually for.

The short answer: start with what is already on your wrist

Apple's built-in Sleep app is the right first pick for most people, and it costs nothing. Set it up on iPhone: open the Health app, tap Get Started under Set Up Sleep, and follow the prompts for your sleep goal, bedtime and wake-up time, Sleep Focus and Track Sleep with Apple Watch. Each morning the Sleep app on the watch shows the time you spent Awake and in REM, Core and Deep sleep, for the last 14 days. With watchOS 26 or later you also get a Sleep Score from 0 to 100, built from duration (50 points), bedtime consistency (30) and interruptions (20). Our step-by-step guide on how to track sleep on Apple Watch covers the setup and the usual reasons a night comes up blank.

So the useful question is not "which app tracks sleep" but "what does Apple's app not do that I want". There are five common answers:

  • More numbers. Heart rate during sleep, a sleep bank, blood oxygen, readiness. That is AutoSleep.
  • A smart alarm that wakes you in light sleep. Apple's alarm rings at the clock time you set. AutoSleep, Pillow and SleepWatch have one.
  • Snore and sleep-talk recordings. Pillow.
  • A recovery score that ties sleep to training. Bevel or Athlytic.
  • A verdict instead of charts. Nularo, our app.

If you want more numbers or a smart alarm

AutoSleep (4.7 stars, about 62,000 ratings) is the most common answer from long-time watch owners. It is $9.99 once, and the listing says "No extra In App Purchases. No subscriptions" and no data upload. It can track on its own or switch to Apple Sleep App Mode, where it takes the nights Apple's app recorded and adds sleep rings, a sleep bank, a Sleep Rating, readiness, overnight blood oxygen, respiration rate, wrist temperature and live tracking during the night. Its smart alarm runs on the watch. It is dense, so our Apple Sleep Score vs AutoSleep comparison explains which of its numbers matter. Family Sharing covers up to six people. Already own it and want out? See AutoSleep alternatives, sorted by why you left.

Pillow (4.4 stars, about 96,000 ratings) is the pick if you want a smart alarm plus recordings. It tracks automatically when you wear the watch to bed or manually with the iPhone, shows last night's report and heart rate on the watch, and records snoring and sleep talking with on-device processing. Unlimited history and advanced analysis sit in Premium, $39.99 a year (a monthly plan is listed at $19.99). The watch app needs watchOS 11 or later.

SleepWatch (4.7 stars, about 328,000 ratings, the most on this list) tracks with the watch or the phone and reports total sleep, restful sleep, sleeping heart rate and its dip, sleep rhythm, disruptions and blood oxygen. Its listing says it contains advertising, and Premium is sold at several prices, from $3.99 to $5.99 a month or $24.99 to $39.99 a year.

Sleep++ is the free, plain option from the makers of Pedometer++. Wear the watch overnight and it reports how long and how restfully you slept, and it can save its analysis to Health. It shows ads, it needs iOS 26 or later, and its 4.1 stars from about 5,700 ratings are the lowest here.

Want an alarm that waits for a set amount of sleep rather than a light phase? That is a different trick, covered in how to wake up after 8 hours of sleep on Apple Watch.

If you train and want recovery

Bevel (4.8 stars, about 18,000 ratings) reads Health and gives free Sleep, Recovery, Strain and Stress scores. Its Sleep Score counts stages, heart rate dip and efficiency, which Apple's score does not. It needs the watch worn overnight and a few weeks to calibrate. Pro is $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year for extras like the AI coach; our Bevel app review has the full split.

Athlytic (4.8 stars, about 11,000 ratings) is built around a morning Recovery score from overnight HRV and resting heart rate against your baseline. Its sleep section adds sleep debt, consistency, latency, efficiency and a nightly target bedtime. Pro is $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year. If you only want the recovery number, see whether Apple Watch has a recovery score first.

Two problems every Apple Watch sleep app shares

Battery. No app can track a night the watch spent on the charger. Apple's advice is to charge to at least 30% before bed and wear the watch for at least 1 hour of sleep; if the battery dies, the night is not tracked. You can get a nudge: on iPhone open the Watch app, tap My Watch, tap Sleep, then turn on Charging Reminders, and the watch reminds you before Wind Down.

Two apps, two versions of one night. If Apple's Sleep and a tracker like Pillow or Sleep++ both write sleep to Health, you get duplicates, and apps that read Health may pick the wrong one. Fix it once: open Health, tap Browse, tap Sleep, scroll to Data Sources & Access, tap Edit, drag your preferred source to the top and tap Done. Apple says the top source takes priority, and that a newly added app is placed above your devices automatically, so check the order after installing anything new.

Expect the totals to differ between apps too: each uses its own method, so 7h 10m in one can be 6h 40m in another. Pick one and follow its trend; our guide to apps that rate your sleep explains why the scores never match. If you came from a phone-on-the-nightstand app, Sleep Cycle alternatives covers that switch. None of these apps is a medical device. If your sleep worries you, talk to a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free sleep app for Apple Watch?

Apple's own Sleep app. It is built in, tracks sleep stages, and on watchOS 26 or later gives a 0 to 100 Sleep Score every morning. Bevel's Sleep Score is also free and counts your sleep stages, and Sleep++ is free with ads.

Is AutoSleep worth it if Apple Watch already tracks sleep?

If you want more than a score, yes. AutoSleep costs $9.99 once with no subscription and can read the nights Apple's Sleep app recorded, then add sleep rings, a sleep bank, readiness, overnight blood oxygen and wrist temperature, plus a smart alarm on the watch. If you only check the score, Apple's app is enough.

Which Apple Watch sleep app has a smart alarm?

AutoSleep has one built into its watch app, Pillow includes Smart Wake-up, and SleepWatch lists a smart alarm. Apple's own sleep schedule alarm rings at the fixed wake-up time you set.

Can I use two sleep apps on Apple Watch at once?

Yes, but if both write sleep to Apple Health you will see two versions of the same night. In the Health app, tap Browse, Sleep, then Data Sources & Access, tap Edit and drag the one you trust to the top. Apple says the source at the top takes priority.

Is Nularo a sleep tracker?

No. Nularo does not run on the watch or record the night. It reads the sleep Apple Watch (or another app) saved to Apple Health and sends a morning verdict with a Sleep score, sleep debt and a creature mood against your own 14-day baseline. It is iPhone only, $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial or $9.99 a month.
