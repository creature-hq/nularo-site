Gamified sleep tracker apps: rewards vs consequences

Updated October 20, 2026 · 7 min read

Nularo reads your Apple Health and turns it into a creature that reacts to how you really slept and moved. Get it

Short answer

Apple's built-in Sleep Score (Apple Watch, watchOS 26 or later) rates each night out of 100 but has no game, streaks or rewards. The main gamified options on iPhone: Pokémon Sleep (free, measures sleep with your phone on the bed or Apple Watch data via Apple Health), SleepTown ($1.99, builds a town when you go to bed on time and stay off your phone) and Nularo (subscription, a creature that reacts to last night's Apple Health data). Each rewards something different: measured sleep length, schedule discipline, or your whole night against your own baseline.

You already know you should go to bed earlier. Knowing has not worked. So you are looking for an app that makes it a game: a streak to protect, a thing to collect, a creature that is disappointed in you. There are a handful worth knowing, and they reward very different things.

What Apple gives you for free (a score, not a game)

If you wear an Apple Watch to bed, you already have a nightly grade. Since watchOS 26, the watch gives each night a Sleep Score out of 100, built from sleep duration (up to 50 points), bedtime consistency (30 points, based on when you fell asleep over the last 13 nights) and interruptions (20 points). It shows up in the Sleep app on the watch, in the Smart Stack, or as a watch face complication. On iPhone, open the Health app, tap Search, then tap Sleep.

The watch also handles the boring setup a game would need. In the Health app, tap Browse, then Sleep, and set a sleep schedule with a bedtime, a wake-up time and a Sleep Goal. Turn on Sleep Focus and your phone quiets down at bedtime. Apple says you need to wear the watch for at least an hour of the night for data to register.

What Apple does not do is turn any of this into a game. There are no sleep streaks, no badges for an early night and no running total of how far behind you are. (If that total is what you want, see whether Apple Watch tracks sleep debt.) Every app below adds that layer on top, and most of them get their data from the same place: Apple Health.

Three ways to gamify sleep

"Gamified sleep tracker" covers apps that work in completely different ways. Before picking one, decide which of these you actually want rewarded:

  • Measured sleep. The app measures how long you slept and turns it into points. Pokémon Sleep works this way. Good if your problem is short nights.
  • Schedule discipline. The app does not measure sleep at all. It checks that you started on time, left your phone alone and got up on time. SleepTown works this way. Good if your problem is scrolling past midnight.
  • Consequences for the whole night. The app reads everything your watch recorded and a character reacts to it. Nularo works this way. Good if points and collections stop working on you by week three.

Pokémon Sleep: points for hours

Pokémon Sleep is the best-known sleep game, and the one most people mean when they search for this. Each morning your sleep becomes a Sleep Score, and that score decides how much you help a giant Snorlax and which Pokémon gather around it. Sleep is also sorted into three styles (Dozing, Snoozing and Slumbering), and different Pokémon show up for different styles, which is the collecting hook.

The scoring is simple and deliberately generous with length. Pokémon's official site says players over 15 get the full 100 points for 8 hours 30 minutes or more. That is a long night by most adults' standards, so expect to see scores in the 70s and 80s even on decent nights.

You can measure sleep three ways: phone face down on the mattress, the Pokémon GO Plus + accessory, or a smartwatch through Apple Health. The Apple Watch route has rules that trip people up, all from Pokémon's own pairing guide:

  • Only nights recorded after you link Pokémon Sleep to Health count. Older nights cannot be imported.
  • The night needs sleep stages and has to be at least 90 minutes long.
  • Nights you edited by hand in Health cannot be imported.
  • Wait until the night has appeared in Health before syncing in the game.

Pokémon is clear that the app is for entertainment and not for detecting or treating any condition. It is free, with an optional Premium Pass subscription. It also has a friend list, covered in sleep tracking with a partner or friends.

SleepTown: punishment for scrolling

SleepTown comes from Seekrtech, the studio behind the Forest focus app, and it borrows the same trick. You set a bedtime goal and a wake-up goal. Before bedtime, you open the app and start constructing a building. After you wake, before your wake-up goal, you shake the phone to reveal what you built. Over weeks the buildings add up to a town.

The consequences are the point. According to its App Store listing, if you do not go to bed at your bedtime goal, you cannot build that night, and if you use your phone during sleep time, the building under construction is destroyed. You can set up to two days off a week so a Saturday lie-in does not end your streak.

Notice what is missing: SleepTown does not measure your sleep. It knows when you opened the app, whether you touched your phone and when you shook it. That makes it a strong tool for one specific problem (the phone in bed) and a weak one for anything else. It can write sleep data to Apple Health. It costs $1.99 on the US App Store.

Where Nularo fits

Full disclosure: Nularo is our app. It is the third kind on the list, the one built on consequences rather than collections.

Nularo reads Apple Health read-only (sleep, sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and SpO2) and you never log anything. Each morning you get a verdict notification and a creature in one of four moods: glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious. The mood is set against your own 14-day baseline, not a fixed number like 8 hours 30 minutes, so a night that is great for you counts as great.

The game parts sit around that creature. The Bedtime Pledge is a promise you make about tonight, and evening Confessions are optional. A Weekly Report Card grades the week. Creature Age, recalculated weekly, turns sleep duration, bedtime consistency, steps, active minutes, resting heart rate and HRV into an age, with a breakdown of which habit added the years. It is a rules-based, for-fun number built from public-health guidelines, not a medical measure; there is more on that in biological age apps for Apple Watch. Does gamifying sleep work?

The best data so far comes from Pokémon Sleep. A University of Tsukuba study published in Sleep Health in 2025 looked at 2,063 people who used Pokémon Sleep alongside the diet app Asken for 90 days. Average total sleep time went from about 5.5 to 6.3 hours, and 45.3% of participants slept longer by the end. That is encouraging, but it is an observational study of people who chose to use both apps, with no comparison group, so it cannot show the game caused the change.

A practical way to choose: pick the app whose reward matches the thing you keep getting wrong. Short nights, try Pokémon Sleep. Phone in bed, try SleepTown. Points stop working on you, try a creature that just tells you how it went. None of these is a treatment for anything, and if your sleep is a real worry, that is one for a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best gamified sleep tracker for Apple Watch?

Pokémon Sleep can use Apple Watch sleep through Apple Health, as long as the night has sleep stages and you link Health before the nights you want counted. Nularo also reads Apple Watch sleep through Apple Health. Neither has its own watch app. SleepTown works from your phone and does not need a watch.

Does Pokémon Sleep work with Apple Watch?

Yes, through Apple Health. Pokémon's own pairing guide says you link Pokémon Sleep to Health, wear the watch to bed with sleep tracking on, and wait for the night to appear in Health before syncing. Nights recorded before you linked, nights without sleep stages, and nights you edited by hand in Health cannot be imported.

Does SleepTown actually track sleep?

Not in the way a watch does. Its App Store listing describes a schedule game: start building before your bedtime goal, do not use your phone during sleep time, and shake the phone after waking. It can write sleep data to Apple Health, but it judges you on timing and phone use, not on sleep stages.

Do gamified sleep apps improve sleep?

The evidence is early. A 2025 University of Tsukuba study of 2,063 people who used Pokémon Sleep and a diet app for 90 days found average total sleep time rose from about 5.5 to 6.3 hours, but it was observational, with no control group. If you are worried about your sleep, talk to a doctor.

Is Nularo a sleep game?

Sort of. There is no collecting or building. A creature reads your Apple Health sleep, movement and heart data each night and sets a mood against your own 14-day baseline, with a Bedtime Pledge, a Weekly Report Card and a Creature Age on top. It is iPhone only and a subscription ($39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month).
