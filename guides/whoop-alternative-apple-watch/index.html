Short answer

If you own an Apple Watch, you do not need a Whoop band to get Whoop-style scores. Series 12 and Ultra 4 have Apple's own Readiness score (0 to 10) in the Readiness app. On any watch, apps read the same HRV, resting heart rate and sleep from Apple Health: Bevel (Recovery, Strain, Sleep free), Athlytic ($29.99 a year), Training Today (free readiness score). What Whoop does, and what your watch already records

Whoop gives you three headline numbers. Recovery is a percentage built from overnight HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate and sleep; Whoop's support pages color it green at 67% and above, yellow from 34% to 66%, and red at 33% and below. Strain tracks how hard your heart worked during the day. Sleep compares what you slept with what you needed.

Your Apple Watch records the raw ingredients for all three: heart rate all day, HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate and sleep stages, written to the Health app on your iPhone. What it does not do on most models is boil them into a single morning verdict. That is the gap every app below fills.

Whoop's US membership, per its membership page in November 2026, is $199 a year for One, $239 for Peak and $359 for Life, with the band included. If you already own the watch, that money buys you mostly the scores, a screenless band and Whoop's own coaching.

Start with what is free on your watch

Readiness, if you have a Series 12 or Ultra 4. watchOS 27 added Apple's own recovery score. Apple's support page describes a 0 to 10 score with four labels: Recover (0 to 1), Pace Yourself (2 to 4), Ready (5 to 7) and Go for it (8 to 10). Open the Readiness app on the watch. It needs your height, weight and age in Health Details, Activity and Sleep set up, Overnight Vitals on, and at least 7 nights of wearing the watch to bed. Our guide to whether Apple Watch has a recovery score explains what goes into it and why older watches do not get it.

Training Load, Apple's strain view. Open the Activity app on the watch, tap the Workload button and turn the Digital Crown. Apple compares the intensity and duration of your workouts over the last 7 days with the previous 28, and places you on a scale from well below to well above. It only counts workouts, not your whole day, so it is closer to a weekly strain trend than Whoop's daily Strain.

Sleep Score. Open the Sleep app on the watch after you wake up. Apple scores 0 to 100 from duration (50 points), bedtime consistency (30) and interruptions (20). It is generous; our guide on why the Apple Sleep Score is always high explains why.

Vitals. The Vitals app compares overnight heart rate, respiratory rate, wrist temperature, blood oxygen (where available) and sleep duration with your typical range, and flags nights when two or more are off. It is not a score, but it is the "something is off today" signal many Whoop users actually rely on.

The apps that rebuild Whoop on Apple Watch

Nularo is our app, and it is the pick if what you liked about Whoop was the morning check, not the training plan. It is an iPhone app with a small creature that reads Apple Health read-only (sleep, sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and SpO2), with no logging. Each morning you get a verdict notification, Sleep, Stress and Energy scores and a running sleep debt. The creature lands in one of four moods, glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious, set against your own 14-day baseline. The Whoop Age equivalent is Creature Age, recalculated weekly from sleep, bedtime consistency, steps, active minutes, resting heart rate and HRV, with a per-habit breakdown of the years added. It is a rules-based number for fun, not a biological age test. The honest limitation: Nularo judges, it does not coach. There is no live strain gauge and no "train hard today" advice. Bevel is the closest full copy of Whoop's dashboard, and the core of it is free. The App Store listing describes a Recovery Score ("your daily readiness, simplified"), a Strain Score adjusted for your sleep and recovery, a Sleep Score, a real-time Stress Score and Energy Bank, a battery that combines all four. Bevel's help center keeps Recovery, Sleep, Strain, Stress and Energy Bank on the free plan. Pro ($14.99 a month or $99.99 a year) adds the AI coach, Biological Age, Health Records and training plans. Our guide to free Bevel alternatives breaks down that split.

Athlytic is the one reviewers most often call a Whoop clone, and the listing backs that up. Every morning it reads overnight HRV and resting heart rate against your own baseline for a Recovery score, tracks cardiovascular load around the clock as Exertion from 0 to 10, and gives you a daily target range based on Recovery. That is the Whoop Strain loop. It also has Athlytic Age, sleep debt, a battery and a journal for caffeine or alcohol. Pro is $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year with a 1-week trial. The listing says an Apple Watch is needed for most features.

Training Today does one thing: a Readiness to Train score from your Apple Health HRV and personal baseline, telling you to push, hold steady or ease off. The free tier includes the score, a recent readiness chart, sleep and resting heart rate. Premium adds workout planning and deeper history.

Gentler Streak is for people who found Whoop's red days stressful. It suggests a walk, a workout or rest each day, rest days keep your streak alive, and its watch workout view counts down to overreaching. It is less a recovery percentage and more a kinder training partner.

All of these use their own math on the same sensors, so the same night can score 58 in one app and 71 in another. Pick one and follow its trend. If the stress line is what you want, our comparison of free stress apps for Apple Watch goes deeper.

What you give up without the band

Be honest with yourself about why Whoop appealed. Three things do not transfer to an Apple Watch app.

  • Always on. Whoop is screenless and charges on your wrist, so it rarely misses a night. An Apple Watch has to come off to charge, and a missed night means a missing score the next morning. Charging it while you shower or get ready keeps most people covered.
  • Whoop's own HRV. Whoop calculates HRV differently from Apple, which is why its Apple Health integration does not export HRV. Apple Watch numbers often look lower; our guide on why Apple Watch HRV looks low explains why that is a measurement difference, not a worse heart.
  • Whoop's coaching and community. The apps above replace the scores, not Whoop's coach or its team features.

You can also keep both. Whoop writes workouts, sleep, heart rate, resting heart rate and respiratory rate into Apple Health, so apps that read Apple Health still see that data.

Frequently asked questions

Can an Apple Watch replace a Whoop?

For most scores, yes. The watch records heart rate, HRV, resting heart rate and sleep, and apps such as Bevel and Athlytic build recovery and strain scores from them. What it cannot copy is the hardware: Whoop is a screenless band you never take off, while the watch needs charging and has a screen.

Is there a free Whoop alternative for Apple Watch?

Yes. Bevel's free plan includes Recovery, Strain, Sleep, Stress and Energy Bank, and Training Today's free tier shows a readiness score from your HRV. On Series 12 and Ultra 4, Apple's Readiness score is built in.

How much does Whoop cost compared with these apps?

Whoop's US membership is $199 a year for One, $239 for Peak and $359 for Life, with the band included. Athlytic Pro is $29.99 a year, Training Today Premium $29.99 a year, Bevel Pro $99.99 a year and Nularo $39.99 a year.

Can I use Whoop and Apple Watch together?

Yes. Whoop's Apple Health integration writes workouts, sleep, heart rate, resting heart rate and respiratory rate to Apple Health. It does not write its HRV, because Whoop calculates HRV differently from Apple.

Is Nularo a Whoop alternative?

Partly. It reads the same Apple Health data and gives you Sleep, Stress and Energy scores each morning, plus a weekly Creature Age. It does not track strain live or tell you how hard to train, so athletes who plan workouts around a number should pick Athlytic or Bevel.
