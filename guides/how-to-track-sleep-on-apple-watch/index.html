You wore your Apple Watch to bed for the first time, woke up expecting a neat chart of your night, and found nothing. Or you have worn it for months and never quite worked out where the sleep data goes. Sleep tracking on Apple Watch takes about two minutes to set up. Here is the current setup, where to read the results, and the four reasons a night comes back blank.

Set up sleep tracking in two minutes

Apple's requirements are short: an iPhone and an Apple Watch on the latest iOS and watchOS, a watch charged to at least 30 percent at bedtime, and at least 1 hour of sleep with the watch on. The setup starts on iPhone.

  1. Open the Health app, tap Browse, tap Sleep, then tap Get Started under Set Up Sleep and tap Next.
  2. Set a Sleep Goal (the hours you want to sleep), then your bedtime and wake-up time.
  3. Choose Sleep Focus settings. Apple says Sleep Focus simplifies your Lock Screen at bedtime and lets you allow certain people or apps to reach you.
  4. Turn on Track Sleep with Apple Watch. This step only appears if the watch was already paired. If you pair it later, you can switch it on afterward (next step).

You can also start on the watch: open the Sleep app, tap Continue, and edit the schedule (Active On days, Wake Up, Bedtime, Alarm). Apple's current setup then offers to turn on overnight Vitals, which are separate from sleep stages.

The switch that actually records sleep. Whichever way you set up, check it once. On iPhone, open the Watch app, tap My Watch, tap Sleep, and make sure Sleep Tracking is on. Turn on Charging Reminders in the same place; Apple says the watch then reminds you to charge before your Wind Down time and tells you when it is full. On the watch itself, the same switches are in Settings, Sleep.

The schedule is not just an alarm. If you are trying to get your bedtime back under control, our guide to fixing your sleep schedule with iPhone and Apple Watch shows how to set one schedule for all seven days.

Wear it right and keep it charged

Two physical things decide whether the night gets recorded well.

Fit. Apple's support page says a loose watch can register extra movement. The watch estimates your stages from motion on your wrist, so a band that slides around can read as a restless night. Snug enough to stay put, loose enough to sleep in.

Battery. If the watch is below 30 percent at bedtime, it prompts you to charge it, and the wake-up greeting in the morning shows how much charge is left. If the battery dies in the night, Apple says sleep data is not tracked. One routine that works is a short charge in the evening (during a shower or dinner), so the watch goes to bed above 30 percent and comes off in the morning for another top-up.

During Wind Down, Sleep Focus turns off the watch display and limits distractions. On the watch, Settings, Sleep has three related options: Turn On at Wind Down (start Sleep Focus automatically, or turn it off to start it yourself from Control Center), Sleep Screen (simplify the watch and iPhone Lock Screen) and Show Time (keep the date and time visible during Sleep Focus).

If you work nights, the same setup works for daytime sleep, with a schedule set for the hours you actually sleep; see Apple Watch for night shift workers.

Where your sleep data shows up

On the watch. Open the Sleep app in the morning. Apple's watch guide says it shows your sleep score, time in each stage, last night's total and your 14-day average. Turn the Digital Crown to scroll through them.

The Sleep Score. On watchOS 26 and later, you get a score from 0 to 100 after you wake and the night has been analyzed. Apple splits it into duration (up to 50 points), bedtime consistency over the previous 13 nights (up to 30) and interruptions (up to 20), with ratings from Very Low (0 to 40) to Very High (96 and up). Tap the Info button in the Sleep app for the breakdown. It does not count stages at all, which is why the Apple Watch Sleep Score is often high even after a night that felt bad. If you want to chase the top of that scale, how to get a 100 sleep score covers what each part rewards.

On iPhone. Open Health, tap Browse, tap Sleep, then tap Sleep again. The graph opens on one night; tap W, M or 6M for longer views, swipe to move back in time, and tap a column for that night's details. Show More Sleep Data adds Amounts (Time Asleep against your Sleep Goal) and Comparisons (heart rate and respiratory rate, plus wrist temperature on Apple Watch Series 8 or later and Ultra models). Our guide to reading the Apple Watch sleep graph explains what Awake, REM, Core and Deep mean and which parts to trust.

What Apple does not show you is the running total of short nights. That takes a two-minute sum, explained in does Apple Watch track sleep debt, or you can paste your numbers into the sleep debt calculator.

Why a night is blank or wrong

Most "my watch didn't track my sleep" problems come down to one of these:

  • Sleep Tracking is off. Apple says you must turn on Track Sleep with Apple Watch to receive sleep data. Check Watch app, My Watch, Sleep. Setting up a bedtime and alarm alone is not enough if this switch is off.
  • The battery died. No charge, no data. Turn on Charging Reminders and charge to 30 percent or more before bed.
  • Less than an hour of wear. Apple's minimum is 1 hour of sleep with the watch on. A nap with the watch on the charger leaves nothing.
  • A kid's watch. Apple says Sleep is not available on watches set up with Apple Watch For Your Kids (formerly Family Setup).

Numbers that look doubled or odd. If another app or a sleep ring also writes sleep to Health, two sources can describe the same night. In Health, tap Browse, Sleep, scroll down and tap Data Sources & Access. Tap Edit and drag the source you trust to the top; Apple says the top source takes priority when several write the same data type. And if you are looking for the old Time in Bed bar, it is gone on recent versions; why Apple Watch time in bed disappeared covers that.

Forgot to wear it? On the Sleep screen in Health, the Add button in the top-right corner lets you enter a night by hand, so your history is not missing a day.

One more setting people ask about: on Apple Watch Series 9 or later, Ultra 2 or later and SE 3, Apple offers sleep apnea notifications (Health, Browse, Respiratory, Set Up under Sleep Apnea Notifications). Apple says the feature needs sleep tracking on and at least 10 nights of wear in 30 days, is for people 18 or older without a diagnosis, and is not intended to diagnose. If you are worried about your breathing at night, talk to a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

Does Apple Watch track sleep automatically?

Only once Sleep Tracking is on. Apple's support page says that to receive sleep data you must turn on Track Sleep with Apple Watch and wear the watch to sleep for at least 1 hour. You set it up once, in Health on iPhone or in the Watch app, and after that you just wear the watch to bed.

Why did my Apple Watch not track my sleep last night?

The usual causes are Sleep Tracking switched off, a battery that died overnight, less than an hour of wear, or a watch set up with Apple Watch For Your Kids, where Sleep is not available. Check Watch app, My Watch, Sleep first, then charge to at least 30 percent before bed.

How much battery does Apple Watch need to track sleep?

Apple says to charge it to at least 30 percent before bed. If it is below 30 percent at bedtime, the watch prompts you to charge it, and if the battery dies, the night is not recorded. Charging Reminders in Watch app, My Watch, Sleep nudges you before Wind Down.

Where do I see my Apple Watch sleep data?

On the watch, open the Sleep app for your Sleep Score, stages, last night's total and your 14-day average. On iPhone, open Health, tap Browse, tap Sleep, then Sleep again, and use D, W, M and 6M for longer views.
