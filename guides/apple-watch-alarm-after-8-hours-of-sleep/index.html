You know you need about 8 hours. What you do not know is when you will actually fall asleep tonight. So you want the obvious thing: an alarm that waits until your watch has counted 8 hours asleep, then taps you. You open the Sleep settings looking for that switch, and it is not there.

The short answer: Apple's alarms ring at a time, not a total

Apple Watch has no built-in alarm that fires after a set amount of sleep. Every alarm Apple gives you is a clock time:

  • The sleep schedule's Wake Up alarm. You set it in the Health app under Browse, Sleep, then Sleep. It rings at the Wake Up time on the curved slider.
  • The Alarms app. Same idea, a fixed time, with optional snooze.

The part that confuses people is the sleep goal. Apple's setup asks for one ("the number of hours you'd like to spend asleep"), so it is natural to assume the alarm uses it. It does not. Apple's support page says the slider "turns orange if the schedule doesn't meet your sleep goal." That is the goal's whole job: it checks your planned bedtime and wake time against each other. It never looks at when you actually fell asleep. If you lie awake for an hour, the alarm still rings at 6:30.

Shortcuts cannot fill the gap either. Apple lists three Sleep triggers for personal automations: Wind Down Begins, Bedtime Begins and Waking Up, which runs when the Wake Up alarm goes off. There is no trigger for "the watch thinks you are asleep," so there is no moment to start an 8-hour countdown from. People have asked Apple for exactly this feature on Apple's community forums for years. It has not shipped as of iOS 27 and watchOS 27.

So the options are a free approximation with Apple's own apps, or a third-party app that watches your sleep and decides for you.

The free workaround: a timer, or tonight's wake time

1. Start a long timer when you lie down. Apple says the Timers app on Apple Watch can track time for up to 24 hours, so an 8-hour timer is well within range. Open the Timers app, scroll down, tap Add, tap hours and minutes and turn the Digital Crown, then tap Start. Faster: raise your wrist and ask Siri to "set a timer for 8 hours 30 minutes." On iPhone, the Clock app's Timers tab does the same, and the When Timer Ends option lets you pick the sound.

2. Add a buffer, because a timer counts time in bed, not time asleep. This is the weak spot. The timer starts when you press Start, not when you drift off, and it keeps running through every wake-up. A reasonable formula:

timer length = sleep goal + time it usually takes you to fall asleep + time you usually spend awake in the night

You can estimate the last part from your own data. In Health, tap Browse, Sleep, then Show More Sleep Data and choose Stages to see time spent Awake. The first part is harder: the watch only records Awake between two stretches of sleep, and Apple stopped writing Time in Bed on watchOS 11, so the minutes before you nod off are not shown anywhere (more on that in why Apple Watch time in bed is missing). Most people guess, then adjust after a week.

3. Or move just tonight's alarm. If you go to bed late, change Next Wake Up Only instead of the full schedule. In Health, tap Browse, Sleep, Sleep, tap Edit under Your Schedule, drag the slider, and tap Done. Apple says this change applies only to the following day. You can also change the next Wake Up alarm from the Clock app. If you are not sure what time to pick, a sleep calculator for bedtimes and wake-up times works it out in whole 90-minute cycles. Shift workers who do this every few days will find the full setup in Apple Watch for night shift workers.

Whatever you choose, keep one fixed alarm at the latest time you can possibly get up. A timer you forgot to start is not an alarm.

Before you let an app decide when you wake up

A duration alarm is only as good as the watch's guess about when you fell asleep. That guess comes from movement and heart data on your wrist, and it can be off. If you lie very still while awake, the watch may start counting early. If a loose band picks up movement, it may count late or log wake-ups that did not happen. Apple's own advice for accurate sleep data applies here too:

  • Charge to at least 30% before bed. Apple says that if the battery dies, the watch won't track your sleep, and a duration alarm that loses tracking at 3am cannot count to 8 hours. Turn on Charging Reminders in the Watch app on iPhone, under My Watch, Sleep.
  • Wear it at a comfortable, snug fit. Apple says a loose watch can register too much movement during sleep.
  • Check what the app writes to Health. Some alarm apps also write their own sleep data, which can double up with your watch. The fix is in Data Sources & Access, explained in our time in bed guide.

The same "time asleep, not time in bed" rule drives the 50 duration points in Apple's Sleep Score, which is why a night you planned as 8 hours can score short. How to get a 100 Sleep Score breaks that down. And if your real question is how many hours you personally need, or you are tired after long nights anyway, that is a question for a doctor rather than an alarm setting.

Frequently asked questions

Can Apple Watch wake me up after a set amount of sleep?

Not with Apple's built-in apps. The Sleep schedule alarm and the Alarms app both ring at a clock time. Your sleep goal is used to check whether your bedtime and wake time leave enough room, not to trigger the alarm.

Can a Shortcut wake me after 8 hours asleep?

No. Apple's Shortcuts Sleep triggers are Wind Down Begins, Bedtime Begins and Waking Up. Waking Up runs when the Wake Up alarm goes off. None of them runs when the watch decides you fell asleep, so there is nothing to start an 8-hour countdown from.

How long can an Apple Watch timer run?

Apple says the Timers app can track time for up to 24 hours, and you can run several timers at once. So an 8 or 9 hour timer started when you lie down works as a rough duration alarm.

Does Oura or Whoop have an alarm based on hours slept?

Oura does not. Its FAQ says the ring has no smart alarm and no sound, light or vibration alerts. Whoop's Sleep Planner alarm has a Sleep Goal option that can wake you once you reach your goal within a one-hour window before your latest wake time, and at the latest time if you do not.

Is Nularo an alarm app?

No. Nularo reads your sleep from Apple Health the morning after and tells you how the night went, including a running sleep debt. It has no alarm and does not work in real time.
