Where to find each number in Apple Health

Average sleep. Open Health on iPhone, tap Browse, tap Sleep, then tap Sleep again. Tap the week tab at the top and tap each night's column for its Time Asleep. Add up the nights you have and divide by how many there are. Skip nights with no data instead of counting them as zero. On Apple Watch, the Sleep app shows Time Asleep for the last 14 days if you turn the Digital Crown. Our sleep debt calculator uses the same nightly figures.

Bedtime spread. On the same week view, each night's column shows when sleep started. Find the earliest and latest start in the week and count the minutes between them. A week of 11:00 pm to 12:35 am is 95 minutes. This is a rough stand-in for the consistency measures that sleep apps score; sleep consistency apps compares how they each do it.

Steps. Tap Browse, then Activity, then Steps, and use the week tab. iPhone counts steps on its own, so this works without a watch, though only while the phone is in your pocket.

Exercise minutes. Tap Browse, then Activity, then Exercise Minutes. These come from Apple Watch, which counts minutes of brisk activity. Add up a typical week. No watch? Estimate the minutes of brisk walking or harder exercise you do.

The rules, shown

Each habit adds or subtracts years from your actual age. The guideline behind each cut-off is public. The size of each step (half a year, one year) is our own round number, chosen to be readable. It is not taken from a study, and no study says a short night costs you exactly a year.

The example (the button fills it in): a 38-year-old sleeping 6 h 40 min (+0.5), with a 95-minute bedtime spread (+0.5), 8,400 steps (−0.5) and 110 exercise minutes a week (0). Total: +0.5. Habit age 38.5. The breakdown says what the single number hides: the steps are fine, the bedtime is the problem.

Why these cut-offs. The CDC lists 7 or more hours of sleep for adults 18 to 60, and 150 minutes a week of moderate-intensity activity (or 75 of vigorous). Sleep over 9 hours is scored neutral, because the guideline sets a floor, not a target above it. For steps, a 2022 meta-analysis of 15 cohorts in The Lancet Public Health by Paluch and colleagues found the risk of death kept falling with more steps up to 6,000 to 8,000 a day in adults 60 and older, and 8,000 to 10,000 in younger adults, which is why the top tier starts at 6,000 after 60. For bedtime, a 2024 UK Biobank study by Windred and colleagues in the journal SLEEP found sleep regularity was a stronger predictor of mortality than sleep duration. Both studies show associations, not cause.

What a real biological age test measures

Search results mix three very different things under one name.

  • Questionnaire calculators (like this one, and most of what ranks) add and subtract years for answers about sleep, exercise, smoking or diet. The weights are the author's. They are useful as a habit summary and nothing more.
  • Blood-based formulas. PhenoAge, published by Morgan Levine and colleagues in the journal Aging in 2018, combines nine routine blood markers (albumin, creatinine, glucose, C-reactive protein, lymphocyte percentage, mean cell volume, red cell distribution width, alkaline phosphatase and white blood cell count) with your age. The formula was fitted to mortality data from the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES III, about 9,900 adults). It needs lab results you cannot get from a watch.
  • Epigenetic clocks estimate age from DNA methylation, chemical marks at specific sites in your genome, measured in a blood or saliva sample. The National Institute on Aging describes epigenetic age as an estimate of biological age based on those changes.

Watch-based ages sit between the first and the others: they use measured data instead of guesses, but the weights are still the app's. Our comparison of biological age apps for Apple Watch covers Whoop Age, Bevel's Biological Age and others. Apple's own Health Age is announced but not out yet; how to see Apple Health Age tracks where it stands. The closest free number today is Cardio Fitness (Browse, Heart, Cardio Fitness), which rates VO2 max against your age and sex, and fitness age on Apple Watch shows how to read an age off it.

None of these numbers is a diagnosis. If a result worries you, talk to a doctor.

Where Nularo fits

Full disclosure: Nularo is our app. Its Creature Age is the automatic version of the calculator above, with two more inputs. It reads Apple Health (read-only) and recalculates every week from sleep duration (anchored at 7h30 a night), bedtime consistency, steps (8,000 a day), active minutes (30 a day), resting heart rate and HRV. There is nothing to type. The Age tab breaks down which habit added the years, so you see that it was the drifting bedtime and not the step count.

Its weights are not the ones in this page's table, so the two numbers will not match. A good stretch can make the creature up to 1.5 years younger than you, and the number stays hidden until there are 7 days of data. Like the calculator, it is a rules-based, for-fun number built from public-health guidelines, not a biological age test and not medical.

The honest limitations: it is iPhone only, and without an Apple Watch there is far less sleep and heart data, so the number gets thinner. It judges, it does not coach.
