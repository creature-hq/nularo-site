Where to find each number in Apple Health

You need two things: your sleep goal and how long you slept each night. Both are in the Health app on iPhone, and the watch can show the nightly figures too.

Your sleep goal. Open Health, tap Browse, tap Sleep, then tap Sleep again. Scroll to Your Schedule, tap Full Schedule & Options, and look under Additional Details for Sleep Goal. If you never set one, pick a reference point. The CDC lists 7 or more hours a night for adults aged 18 to 60. That is a general guideline, not your personal need.

Each night's sleep. On the same Sleep screen (Browse, Sleep, Sleep), tap the week tab at the top of the chart and tap each night's column. The figure you want is Time Asleep, the sleep the watch recorded, not the span from bedtime to alarm. Swipe the chart left to go back a week if you are counting 14 nights. Our guide to reading the Apple Watch sleep graph explains the stages and Awake gaps you will see on each night.

On the watch. Open the Sleep app on Apple Watch and turn the Digital Crown. Apple says it shows Time Asleep, sleep stages and sleep duration for the last 14 days, which is exactly the window the 14-night mode needs.

Two things to check before you trust the result:

  • Nights the watch missed. Apple asks for at least 30% charge at bedtime with Sleep Tracking turned on, and at least an hour of wear. A night on the charger shows no data. Leave it blank so the calculator skips it, because entering 0 adds a whole night's goal to your debt.
  • Daytime sleep. If you sleep after night shifts, check that those sleeps landed in Health at all. The guide to Apple Watch for night shift workers covers schedules for that. And if you are looking for the old In Bed figure, Apple stopped recording it; see why time in bed is missing.

The math, shown

The formula every calculator uses is the same at its core: sleep goal times the nights counted, minus the sleep you got. Here is the example the button fills in, with a 7 hour 30 minute goal:

Six nights add up to 43 hours 25 minutes. Six times 7 hours 30 minutes is 45 hours. Net debt: 1 hour 35 minutes.

Now count only the short nights and ignore the two long ones: 50 + 25 + 75 + 35 minutes is 3 hours 5 minutes. Same week, nearly double the number. Calculators differ on this. Rise's own calculator says sleep beyond your need pays some of the total down, and Omni's weekly version subtracts the whole week's sleep, so a long night lowers it too. The calculator above shows both so you can see how much of your week a single sleep-in is covering.

And the blank night matters most. Count it as zero and the debt jumps to 9 hours 5 minutes (7 times 7 hours 30 minutes is 52 hours 30 minutes, minus 43 hours 25 minutes). A watch on the charger is not a night without sleep.

Social jet lag is a different sum: it compares when you sleep on work nights and free nights, not how much. That one is in how to calculate social jet lag. Why sleep debt apps give you a different number

If you already use an app and its number does not match yours, it is usually the window, the target or the weighting, not an error. From each app's own App Store listing or website:

  • Rise totals the gap over the last 14 nights against a personal sleep need it estimates for you, rather than a fixed goal. Rise says last night counts for 15% of the total and the 13 nights before it make up the other 85%, with recent nights weighted more.
  • Sleep Ledger: Debt Tracker uses a 14-day rolling window from your Apple Health sleep, and its listing says the debt is quality-weighted: it uses Apple Watch sleep stages (Deep, REM, Core), so two nights of the same length can count differently.
  • AutoSleep shows a sleep bank balance next to Time Asleep. It is a one-time $9.99 purchase with no subscription.
  • Athlytic lists sleep debt in its sleep section and uses it, with recovery and exertion, to set a nightly sleep target and bedtime.

So the same Apple Health data can give four different totals. Pick one method and watch its direction over time, rather than comparing one app's number against another's. Prices and what each app needs are in our guide to whether Apple Watch tracks sleep debt. Apple's own Sleep Score is not a debt at all: it rates one night out of 100, which is why a good score can sit on top of a bad week; see what a 100 Sleep Score takes.

None of these numbers is a diagnosis. A sleep debt figure says how far your recorded sleep fell from a target you chose. If your sleep worries you, talk to a doctor.

Where Nularo fits

Full disclosure: Nularo is our app. It does this sum for you every morning without the typing. It reads your sleep from Apple Health (read-only) and shows a 7-day sleep debt on its Overview tab, next to last night's bedtime, wake time and sleep quality. In the screenshot it reads DEBT 7D, 2h 24m.

The number comes with an opinion. A creature lives on your Health data, and its mood (glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious) is set against your own 14-day baseline, with a morning verdict notification written from last night's real numbers. There is a Bedtime Pledge in the evening and a Weekly Report Card at the end of the week.

The honest limitation: it judges, it does not coach, so you get the debt and a raised eyebrow, not a plan for paying it back. It uses a fixed 7-day window, so it will not match a 14-night app. It is iPhone only, it needs a watch on at night for good sleep data, and it updates the morning after rather than in real time.
