It is Black Friday, WHOOP has a sale on, and your renewal email says the next year is coming whether you think about it or not. Before you pay again, or for the first time, here is what the subscription actually costs, what happens when it renews, and how to get out cleanly.

What a WHOOP subscription costs in 2026

WHOOP does not sell the band on its own. You buy a membership and the hardware comes with it. WHOOP's billing support page lists three 12-month plans in the US:

Plan12 months, upfrontMonthly, after year one
WHOOP One$199$25
WHOOP Peak$239$30
WHOOP Life$359$40

Three details matter more than the sticker price:

  • It is paid upfront and renews by itself. Auto-renewal is on by default. WHOOP says it sends a renewal reminder 30 days before your billing date, so that email is your deadline to decide.
  • Monthly billing is not a starting option. WHOOP's pricing page says monthly subscriptions are only open to members who have completed their 12-month commitment. New members can also choose a 24-month term at signup.
  • The free trial is a Peak membership in waiting. The trial is one month with a certified pre-owned WHOOP 5.0. If you do nothing, it converts to a 12-month Peak membership at $239 a year. If you cancel without sending the device back, WHOOP charges a $110 restocking fee, and cancelling a trial ends access right away.

Over three years, the cheapest plan at the listed price is $597 and the top one is $1,077. That is the number to hold in your head while a sale banner tells you how much you are saving.

Black Friday and renewals: what is actually confirmed

WHOOP runs a sale around Black Friday every year, and the same question shows up in several separate Reddit threads this week: can an existing member renew at the sale price? WHOOP's public pages do not say. Here is what WHOOP itself has confirmed in writing:

  • Last year's Cyber Sale, per a WHOOP staff reply on the official community forum in December 2025, put a 12-month Life membership at $299, $60 under the regular price.
  • The same reply said a trial member could upgrade to Life at the sale price by extending 12 months, and that promotions cannot be combined or stacked.

Sale prices and terms change each year, so read the price in the Membership section at app.whoop.com on the day, and if you already have a membership, ask Membership Services in writing whether the offer extends your current term before you pay. A discount on a longer commitment is still a longer commitment.

How to cancel, pause or get a refund

Cancel. WHOOP says you do not need to contact support. Open the Membership section at app.whoop.com, scroll to the bottom and choose Cancel your Membership. You get a cancellation confirmation; keep it. An annual membership does not stop on the spot. It stays active until the end of the term you paid for, then simply does not renew. Monthly members still inside a commitment can pay off the remaining months or schedule the cancellation for the end of the commitment.

Refunds. Within 30 days you can cancel and return the device from app.whoop.com/membership (Membership & Billing, then Cancel, then generate a return label). WHOOP says refunds are processed within 10 business days after the return arrives and is inspected. After 30 days, WHOOP does not refund prepaid memberships.

Pause. If you only need a break, WHOOP allows a pause of up to 3 consecutive months. You are not billed and no data is uploaded. The catches: you cannot be inside a commitment period, you can pause once every 12 months, Family Plan members cannot pause, and Life (MG) members may have different rules. If you decide to cancel while paused, you have to unpause first.

Before you go, export. WHOOP staff say that once a membership ends, WHOOP stops collecting, uploading and analyzing your data. To keep a copy, open the WHOOP app on iPhone, tap More, then App Settings, then Data Export, and tap Create Export. WHOOP emails a download link within 24 hours, with CSV files covering recovery, resting heart rate, HRV, strain and sleep.

The cheaper route if you already wear an Apple Watch

A lot of WHOOP members also own an Apple Watch, which records heart rate, HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate and sleep stages into the Health app. What it lacks on most models is one morning verdict, and that is the part the subscription mostly buys. Some of it is free:

  • Readiness on Series 12 and Ultra 4 with watchOS 27: a 0 to 10 score built in. Our guide to whether Apple Watch has a recovery score covers what feeds it.
  • Sleep Score in the watch's Sleep app, 0 to 100 from duration, bedtime consistency and interruptions. Set it up with our guide on how to track sleep on Apple Watch.
  • Vitals, which flags nights when two or more overnight readings are outside your typical range.

Nularo is our app, so take this with that in mind. It is an iPhone app with a small creature that reads Apple Health read-only (sleep, sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and SpO2), with no logging. Each morning you get a verdict notification, Sleep, Stress and Energy scores and a running sleep debt, and the creature lands in one of four moods (glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious) set against your own 14-day baseline. Once a week it recalculates Creature Age, a rules-based, for-fun number from sleep, bedtime consistency, steps, active minutes, resting heart rate and HRV. The honest limitation: it judges, it does not coach. There is no live strain gauge and no "push hard today". It costs $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month.

If training targets are the reason you have WHOOP, Athlytic (Pro $29.99 a year or $4.99 a month on the US App Store) rebuilds the Recovery and Exertion loop, and Bevel keeps Recovery, Strain and Sleep on its free plan, with Pro at $99.99 a year or $14.99 a month. Our Bevel app review and the full comparison of WHOOP alternatives for Apple Watch go through each one.

What you give up is real: WHOOP is screenless and charges on your wrist, so it rarely misses a night, while a watch has to come off to charge. And WHOOP's HRV is calculated differently, so do not compare old WHOOP numbers with new Apple ones; our guide on why Apple Watch HRV looks low explains why.

Switching without losing your history

If you connected WHOOP to Apple Health, it has been writing workouts, sleep, heart rate, resting heart rate and respiratory rate there (not HRV). That data does not disappear when your membership ends. Apple's support page on managing Health data says that turning off a source stops it from adding new data to a category, and existing records stay until you delete them.

To make Apple Watch the main sleep source, open Health on iPhone, tap Browse, then Sleep, scroll to Data Sources & Access, tap Edit and drag Apple Watch to the top. Then give any new app one to two weeks: they all compare you with your own baseline, so the first scores are rough.

None of these, WHOOP included, is a medical device. If a number worries you, talk to a doctor.
