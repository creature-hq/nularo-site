You wake up, open the Health app and look for last night's HRV. You find a chart with dots scattered across the whole day, maybe two of them at night, and nothing labelled "sleep". Your friend's ring gives one tidy overnight number. So does your watch track HRV while you sleep at all?

Yes, but how much depends on your watch

Every Apple Watch that records heart rate variability can record it while you sleep. The difference between models is how often.

On most watches, HRV is not measured continuously. Apple's support page on heart rate says HRV is sampled while you are still, so the number of measurements varies with your activity level. Night is the stillest stretch of your day, so it is where many readings land, but there is no fixed schedule. In Apple Community threads, owners describe getting only a few readings a night, at irregular times. HRV researcher Marco Altini has written that the watch now logs a few more overnight points than it used to, but still nothing like a full-night recording.

Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4 are the exception. Apple's September 2026 announcement says HRV is now measured as often as every five minutes on those models, about 24 times more often than before. They also split HRV into two readings: Recovery HRV, which Apple says is best for daily signals of stress and recovery, and overall HRV, for longer-term trends. Overnight Vitals on those models compares Recovery HRV against your personal baseline. Bevel's help center says the Series 12 and Ultra 4 measure Recovery HRV using rMSSD, the formula Whoop and Oura use; Apple's own press material does not name the formula.

Whatever the model, your watch can only record what it sees. Apple's sleep tracking needs Sleep set up on iPhone with Track Sleep with Apple Watch and Sleep Focus on, and the watch on your wrist all night.

Where to find your HRV from last night

There is no "sleep HRV" chart in the Health app. You find it by looking at the hours you were asleep.

  1. Open the HRV chart. On iPhone, open Health, tap Browse, tap Heart, then tap Heart Rate Variability.
  2. Switch to D. The day view shows readings by hour. The dots between your bedtime and wake time are your sleep HRV.
  3. Get exact times. Scroll down to Options and tap Show All Data. Each reading is listed with its time, so you can match them against the sleep times in the Sleep chart.
  4. Check Vitals on Series 12 or Ultra 4. Open the Vitals app on the watch. Apple's support page says that on those two models you see HRV in place of sleep duration in the overnight view, plus a daytime view with resting heart rate and HRV. Vitals needs about 7 nights of sleep tracking to build your typical range.

On older watches, Vitals shows heart rate, respiratory rate, blood oxygen, wrist temperature and sleep duration overnight, but not HRV. That is the most common reason people think their watch is not tracking it. The data is in the HRV chart in Health, not in Vitals.

If you want to see how those dots line up with your sleep stages, our guide to reading the Apple Watch sleep graph explains what each band means.

Why a few readings a night make a jumpy number

HRV is not one steady value across the night. Altini points out that it shifts with sleep stage and with the time of night, so a reading taken in deep sleep at 1am and one taken in REM at 5am can be far apart for reasons that have nothing to do with how you slept. When a watch takes three readings, which three it happened to take matters a lot.

That is why a night-to-night comparison of single readings on an older watch is noisy. A few things make it more useful:

  • Zoom out. Switch the HRV chart to W or M. A weekly or monthly range is more meaningful than any one night.
  • Compare like with like. Apple Watch stores its standard HRV as SDNN, a different formula from the rMSSD most rings and bands use, so the scales differ. Our guide to why Apple Watch HRV looks low covers that gap in detail.
  • Keep the fit consistent. Apple's accuracy advice is a snug but comfortable band above the wrist bone. A watch that slides around in your sleep misses readings.

As for "what is a good HRV while sleeping": there is no universal number that applies across people and devices, and this page will not invent one. Your own range, built over weeks, is the reference. If your readings sit far outside it for a long stretch, or you feel unwell, talk to a doctor and bring the chart.

Getting one nightly sleep HRV number

If you want a single overnight HRV figure each morning, the way a Whoop or Oura shows it, a few iPhone apps calculate one from the readings your watch wrote to Apple Health.

  • Bevel. Its help center lets you choose which readings count: go to Profile, Customization, Calculations, then Use HRV from. The options are Entire Sleep (marked recommended), Entire Sleep + Mindfulness, and Entire Day. You can also switch the HRV Method between rMSSD and SDNN. Bevel notes that using Entire Day makes its Recovery score keep changing through the day. Our guide to free Bevel alternatives covers which features are free.
  • Athlytic. Its App Store listing says its sleep section includes overnight charts of HRV, heart rate, respiratory rate and blood oxygen, and that it reads your overnight HRV each morning against your own baseline. On Series 12 and Ultra 4 it uses Apple's Recovery HRV, and its App Store update notes say you can switch Recovery back to its own rMSSD calculation or to SDNN.

These apps cannot add readings your watch did not take. On an older watch, a "sleep HRV" average is still an average of a handful of points. Both also roll HRV into a morning recovery score, which our guide to whether Apple Watch has a recovery score compares with Apple's own Readiness.

Where Nularo fits

Nularo is an iPhone app with a small creature that lives on your Apple Health data. It reads HRV and resting heart rate read-only, along with sleep, sleep stages, steps, active minutes and workouts, and you never log anything. Each morning it turns your night into Sleep, Stress and Energy scores, and the creature's mood (glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious) is set against your own 14-day baseline, not a chart of strangers.

The Overview tab above puts HRV and resting heart rate next to sleep debt, Stress and Energy, so a low reading sits beside the short night that may explain it. If you work nights and your sleep happens in daylight, our guide to sleep apps for night shift workers covers how that data is recorded.

The honest limitation: Nularo can only use the HRV readings your watch already wrote to Apple Health. On an older Apple Watch that means the same few overnight points described above, and with no watch on at night there is little to work with. It updates the morning after, not in real time, and it judges rather than coaches, so it will not tell you what to do about a low number. It is iPhone only and costs $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month.
