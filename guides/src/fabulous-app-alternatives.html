Fabulous got you drinking a glass of water every morning for two weeks. Then the coaching letters piled up unread, the routine felt like homework you had to tap through, and the renewal email arrived. You still want the routine. You want a different app to hold it.

What Fabulous does well, and why people leave

Credit first. Fabulous holds 4.4 stars from about 90,000 ratings on the US App Store and had an update on November 16, 2026. Its listing says it started as a habit tracker and grew into a coaching and self-care platform: guided morning and evening routines, daily coaching series, a journal, short workouts, breathing exercises, affirmations, live challenges, and paid one-on-one coaching. It runs on iPhone and Apple Watch. For a lot of people, the guided "journey" that adds one habit at a time is exactly what gets a routine started.

People who leave usually hit one of four walls:

  • The price and the billing. The download is free, but the App Store lists ten Premium purchases, from $16.99 to $59.99, including a $49.99 annual subscription. Which offer you see depends on when and where you sign up. Apple Community threads keep asking why a Fabulous charge is not in their Apple subscriptions (answer below).
  • Everything is manual. The listing never mentions Apple Health. Every step is a tap, so the tapping becomes the habit you are failing at.
  • Too much content. Some people wanted a checklist and a timer, not letters, audio and journeys.
  • It is heavy. The iPhone app is 683.6 MB.

Full disclosure: we make Nularo, which is second in the table below. We kept the rest fair and say plainly where Nularo is the wrong pick.

Before you leave: cancel Fabulous the right way

Deleting the app does not stop billing. Where you cancel depends on where you subscribed, and Fabulous sells both through the App Store and on its own website.

  1. Check your iPhone first. Open Settings, tap your name, then tap Subscriptions. If Fabulous is listed, tap it, then tap Cancel Subscription (you may need to scroll down). If there is no Cancel button, or you see an expiration date in red, it is already cancelled.
  2. Not listed? You subscribed on the web. Apple's own support page says a subscription missing from that list was likely bought from another company, which has to cancel it. Fabulous's help center says a confirmation email from noreply@thefabulous.co means you subscribed directly with them.
  3. Cancel the web plan. Per Fabulous's subscription terms, sign in at thefabulous.co and go to Account, then Manage subscription, then Cancel subscription. Or contact Member Care at least 24 hours before your renewal date. Fabulous says there is no cancellation fee and you keep access until the paid period ends.

Two more details from Fabulous's help center. A free trial converts to a paid plan unless you cancel at least 24 hours before it ends; cancelling on the last day may not stop the charge. And a $1 charge on your card is a temporary verification hold, not a payment. Refunds depend on where you subscribed: Apple handles App Store purchases, Fabulous handles web ones.

If tapping through every step got old

Streaks. The plain, fast answer. Pick up to 24 habits; with your permission, Streaks completes them from more than 80 types of Apple Health data, such as steps, workouts, sleep, water and mindful minutes. It runs on iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Mac, holds 4.8 stars from about 27,000 ratings, and costs $5.99 once. No coaching, no subscription.

Nularo. This is our app, and it is built for the person whose routine was really about sleeping and moving more. There is nothing to tick. Nularo reads Apple Health (read-only): sleep and sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and blood oxygen. Each morning a verdict notification arrives and the creature is in one of four moods: glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious, measured against your own 14-day baseline. The evening half of a Fabulous routine becomes a Bedtime Pledge: you promise a time, and the creature notices whether you kept it. Creature Age, recalculated weekly from sleep, bedtime consistency, steps, active minutes, resting heart rate and HRV, shows which habit is adding years. It is a for-fun, rules-based number, not a medical one.

The honest limits. Nularo only knows what Apple Health records, so "drink water" or "journal for five minutes" are invisible to it, and without an Apple Watch the sleep and heart data is thin. It is iPhone only and works the morning after, not in real time. It judges, it does not coach: no letters, journeys or audio. And it is a subscription with a hard paywall after onboarding, $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial or $9.99 a month, billed through the App Store, so it always shows up in Settings.

Habitify. A polished tracker that logs progress from Apple Health, Screen Time, Fitbit and Strava, with charts and challenges (the Apple Health sync is part of Pro). It holds 4.6 stars from about 7,200 ratings. Our habit tracker app comparison lines up every automatic and manual option.

If you liked the routines but not the coaching

Routinery is the closest swap for Fabulous's morning routine. You string small habits into a routine, each step gets its own timer, and a voice tells you what comes next. It has widgets, shared routines and streaks, and holds 4.7 stars from about 18,000 ratings. The free plan allows two routines with ads; Premium removes both, listed at $36 a year or $5 a month. Its FAQ says there is no direct sync with Apple Health.

Structured puts tasks, calendar events and routines on one visual timeline. The free version covers the timeline, an inbox, a focus timer, widgets and Apple Watch sync. Pro adds calendar connection, recurring routines and automatic replanning, sold monthly, yearly or as a $99.99 lifetime purchase. It is an Editors' Choice pick with 4.8 stars from about 169,000 ratings.

Tiimo is a visual planner designed with neurodivergent users in mind: a daily timeline, a focus timer with Apple Watch countdowns, and calendar sync. The free version includes the basic planner and one profile; the 7-day Pro trial applies to the yearly plan only. Also an Editors' Choice pick, with 4.6 stars from about 22,000 ratings.

Apple's free option. If what you used most was the evening routine, the Health app already has one. Open Health, tap Browse, tap Sleep, then Sleep again, scroll to Your Schedule and tap Full Schedule & Options. Tap Wind Down and choose how long before bedtime it starts. Sleep Focus turns on at the start of that period. Our guide to fixing your sleep schedule covers the rest of the setup, and revenge bedtime procrastination explains why the evening is where routines usually break.

If the self-care side was what you loved

Finch keeps Fabulous's gentle tone and swaps the coach for a small bird. You set tiny goals, check in on your mood, and your pet goes on adventures when you do them. Missing a day does not hurt it. It is free with optional Finch Plus and holds 4.9 stars from about 780,000 ratings. Our Finch app review covers it in full, Finch alternatives lists the options when it gets too sweet, and tamagotchi apps for self care rounds up other gentle pets.

If streaks themselves stressed you, (Not Boring) Habits has no streaks and no guilt by design: the goal is 60 repetitions of a habit, and a missed day costs nothing. It holds 4.8 stars from about 6,400 ratings and is free, with an optional Plus tier. And if you came to Fabulous for its game-like journeys, Habitica and its alternatives go much further down that road.
