It is Sunday. You have not left your bed since you woke up at nine, and it is now 4 PM. There are snack wrappers, a laptop on its third episode, and a phone at 12%. You are not sick and you are not sleeping. The internet has a name for this, and your Apple Watch has a surprisingly precise record of it.

What bed rotting means

Bed rotting is slang for spending long stretches in bed on purpose when you are neither asleep nor ill. Usually it is a full day or a weekend, with the bed as the base for everything else: scrolling, streaming, eating, texting, staring at the ceiling. The term spread on TikTok, and news outlets started covering it as a trend in 2023.

The people who post about it frame it as rest. A day with no plans, no productivity and no guilt, after weeks that asked too much. The name is a joke at its own expense. Nobody claims rotting sounds healthy.

It is worth separating from things that look similar:

  • It is not sleeping in. Sleeping late is sleep. Bed rotting is the hours you are awake in bed afterwards.
  • It is not revenge bedtime procrastination. That is staying up late at night to win back free time. Bed rotting happens in daylight. They can feed each other, though: a late night makes a day in bed more tempting. Our guide to revenge bedtime procrastination covers the night-time version.
  • It is not bed rest. People who are ill, recovering or living with a disability may need to be in bed. This trend is about choosing it when you do not.

How common it is

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine has asked about it in its Sleep Prioritization Survey. In the 2025 round (2,007 US adults, surveyed online by Atomik Research from June 5 to 13, 2025), 18% said they had tried bed rotting, which the AASM described as staying in bed for extended periods of time. That rose to 31% of Gen Z and fell to 5% of adults 65 and over. Overall, 56% said they had tried at least one of that year's viral sleep trends.

These are people describing their own habits, so they show how widespread the idea is, not what it does to anyone's sleep.

What sleep specialists say about it is fairly consistent, and fairly relaxed about the occasional day. Fiona Barwick of Stanford told Scientific American that one day of bed rotting "is not going to be a big deal." Her concern is habit. Sleep specialists like the bed to be strongly tied to sleep, and a day spent awake in it on TikTok weakens that cue. The same article points to two other things a day in bed changes: you get very little daylight, which helps set your body clock, and very little movement, which helps build sleep pressure for the night.

None of that is a verdict on you. And if staying in bed stops feeling like a choice, if you cannot get up rather than not wanting to, that is a conversation for a doctor, not a trend.

What a bed rot day looks like in Apple Health

Here is the part none of the trend articles cover. If you wear an Apple Watch, it does not record bed rotting as sleep, but it records almost everything else about the day. Three places show it clearly.

Steps. Open the Health app, tap Browse, tap Activity, then Steps. Use the tabs at the top of the chart to switch between week and month views. A bed rot day is usually the lowest bar in the week by a wide margin, often a few hundred steps, all of them to the kitchen and back.

The Stand ring. Apple's blue Stand ring counts how many hours in the day you stood and moved for at least one minute. Unlike steps, it cannot be made up with one walk in the evening, because it counts hours, not totals. A day in bed leaves it mostly empty, which makes it the most honest record of the day.

Time in Daylight. On a recent Apple Watch, tap Browse, then Other Data, then Time in Daylight. The watch uses its ambient light sensor to estimate minutes spent outdoors, and Apple notes it needs Motion Calibration & Distance turned on. A day under the duvet with the curtains shut usually reads close to zero.

What it does not show. The Sleep chart (Browse, Sleep) only draws sleep stages and the awake moments between them. Apple stopped recording In Bed time on watchOS 11, so six hours awake in bed leave no trace there; our guide to why Apple Watch time in bed is missing explains the change. If you dozed off in the afternoon, whether that nap was recorded depends on your settings, and many people find unscheduled naps do not appear.

The night after is worth a look too. On watchOS 26 or later, the Sleep Score gives 30 of its 100 points to bedtime consistency over the last 13 nights, so if the day in bed pushed your sleep time later, it shows up there. The Apple Watch sleep graph guide shows how to read that night.

Where Nularo fits

Full disclosure: Nularo is our app. It will not stop you bed rotting, and it will not lecture you for it. It will notice.

Nularo reads Apple Health read-only (sleep, sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and SpO2), so there is nothing to log. Each morning it sends a verdict notification, and a creature takes one of four moods, glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious, set against your own 14-day baseline. One bed rot Sunday against a normal fortnight earns a raised eyebrow. A whole week of them is a different mood.

Over the week, steps (against 8,000 a day) and active minutes (against 30 a day) feed the weekly Creature Age, alongside sleep, bedtime consistency, resting heart rate and HRV, with a breakdown of which habit added the years. It is a rules-based, for-fun number built from public-health guidelines, not a medical measure. The Weekly Report Card then grades the week, and roast cards are there if you would rather share the verdict than hide it.

The honest limitations: Nularo cannot see what you did in bed, only that you did not move much. It does not read Screen Time. It is morning-after, not real time, and it judges rather than coaches. It is iPhone only, has much thinner data without a watch, and is a subscription with a paywall after onboarding: $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month.

Rotting with the iPhone you already have

If you are going to rot, you can do it with your eyes open. None of this is advice about your health, just the free settings people use to keep a rest day a rest day.

Make it a choice you can see. Check Steps and the Stand ring at the end of the week. One empty day in a full week is a rest day. Several in a row is a pattern, and it is easier to notice on a chart than from inside the duvet.

Keep the night separate. If the evening scroll is what follows you from bed rot day into bedtime, a Screen Time schedule or Downtime with the block switched on helps. Our guide to stopping doomscrolling before bed has both setups. A fixed wake time in your Health sleep schedule keeps a lazy Sunday from becoming a late Monday; how to fix your sleep schedule walks through it.

Tell someone. A rest day you announce is a rest day. One you hide tends to become two. If you want a friend in the loop without sharing numbers, free accountability buddy apps compares the options, and the opposite camp, people optimizing every hour of sleep, is covered in sleepmaxxing apps.
