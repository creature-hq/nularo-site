For a few weeks, Habitica worked. Your avatar levelled up, your party beat a boss, and "drink water" earned you gold. Then you missed three Dailies on a busy week, lost a level and your best sword, and the app went quietly into a folder on page four.

Why people outgrow Habitica (and what it still does best)

Credit first. Habitica is free, open source, run by a small team with volunteer help, and still actively updated (the iPhone app had a release on November 9, 2026). It holds 4.3 stars from about 3,700 ratings on the US App Store. The optional subscription is $4.99 a month or $47.99 a year. Its best feature has always been the party: during a boss quest, a Daily you miss damages everyone, not just you, so your friends have a reason to nag.

Most people who leave hit one of four walls, and each comes from how the game is built:

  • Everything is manual. Habitica's listing never mentions Apple Health. If you tick it, it happened; if you forget to tick it, it did not. After a month, the logging becomes the habit you are failing at.
  • The punishment. Run out of health and, per the Habitica wiki, you lose a level, all your gold and a random piece of equipment. Motivating for some, a reason to never open the app again for others.
  • The community shrank. Habitica removed the Tavern and all Guilds on August 8, 2023. Parties and Challenges remain, but the big public spaces are gone.
  • The pixels. Some people simply outgrow the retro RPG look.

If damage was the only problem, try one thing before you leave: on iPhone, open the menu, tap the gear icon for Settings, and use Pause Damage during a rough week. If it was any of the others, here is what to try instead, grouped by the reason.

If ticking boxes got old: apps that fill themselves in

Streaks. The plain, fast answer. Pick up to 24 habits; with your permission, Streaks completes them from more than 80 types of Apple Health data, including steps, workouts, sleep, water and mindful minutes. It runs on iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Mac, and you can share a habit with a friend who also uses Streaks so they can keep you accountable, which is the closest thing here to a Habitica party. It is an Apple Design Award winner and costs $5.99 once, with no subscription. There is no game at all.

Nularo. Full disclosure, this is our app, and it is the one built for people who liked Habitica's character but hated the checklist. There is nothing to tick. Nularo reads Apple Health (read-only): sleep and sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and blood oxygen. Each morning a verdict notification arrives and the creature is in one of four moods: glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious. The mood is measured against your own 14-day baseline, so a good day for you counts as good. Instead of XP there is Creature Age, recalculated weekly from your sleep, bedtime consistency, steps, active minutes, resting heart rate and HRV, with a breakdown of which habit added the years. It is a for-fun, rules-based number, not a medical one.

The honest limits. Nularo only knows what Apple Health records, so "read 20 pages" or "clean the kitchen" are invisible to it, and without an Apple Watch the sleep and heart data is thin. It is iPhone only and works the morning after, not in real time. It judges, it does not coach. And it is a subscription with a hard paywall after onboarding: $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month. Friend mode replaces the party: a friend sees your mood word and a good-day count, never your health numbers (more in sleep tracking with a partner or friends).

Habitify. A polished tracker that logs progress on its own from Apple Health, Screen Time, Fitbit and Strava, with charts and Challenges (the Apple Health sync is part of Pro). Our habit tracker app comparison lines up every auto and manual option. Free to download; Pro is $8.99 a month or $49.99 a year.

If you still want the RPG: Habit Hunter

If the game was never the problem, Habit Hunter is the closest swap on iPhone. You create a hero, treat tasks as quests, earn XP and gold, buy weapons, armor and skills, and defeat monsters by keeping streaks alive. It adds a built-in Pomodoro timer whose focus sessions feed your RPG progress, and social quests with friends. It holds 4.6 stars from about 800 ratings. The download is free; the App Store lists optional purchases including a $24.99 annual plan and a $29.99 one-time Ultimate version, plus coins and gems. Like Habitica, you check everything off yourself. For game-style options aimed at moving more, see gamified fitness apps.

If the damage stressed you: (Not Boring) Habits or Finch

(Not Boring) Habits is Habitica's opposite. Its listing says it plainly: no streaks, no guilt. The goal is 60 repetitions of a habit, and missing a day costs you nothing. It is an Apple Design Award winner from a small studio, collects no data according to its privacy label, and is free, with !Habits Plus at $14.99 a year or $59.99 lifetime.

Finch keeps the creature and drops the combat. You set tiny goals, your bird gains energy when you do them, and missing a day does not hurt it. It is free, with optional Finch Plus, and holds 4.9 stars from roughly 778,000 ratings. It is very cute, which is the point or the problem. Our Finch app review covers it in full, and Finch alternatives lists what people use when it gets too sweet. For more gentle pets, see tamagotchi apps for self care.

If your phone is the real problem: Forest

Some people used Habitica mostly to fight procrastination. Forest is more direct: start a timer, stay off your phone, and a tree grows; leave early and it withers. You can block distracting apps during sessions, plant with friends in shared sessions, and save your focus time to Apple Health. The developer funds real trees through Trees for the Future. It holds 4.8 stars from about 50,000 ratings. The download is free and Forest Plus is optional (the US App Store lists it at $5.99 a month or $35.99 a year).

Habitvs is worth a look if you want a pet that eats Apple Health data for free. Its creature, Kova, reacts to sleep, movement, HRV, mindfulness and daylight, with "grace weeks" so one bad week does not wipe your streak. It is new, with no App Store ratings yet. We compare it with other data-fed pets in virtual pet apps that use Apple Health.

And if what you really miss is the party, Apple's Fitness app has free 7-day competitions with friends, scored on your Activity rings. We cover that and other free options in free accountability buddy apps.
