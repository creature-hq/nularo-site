You liked the mornings: open the app, see which Pokémon wandered in overnight, feed Snorlax. What you did not like was the phone face down on the mattress, plugged in, all night. Or the Premium Pass. Or the slow realization that you were playing a collecting game more than looking at your sleep.

Why people look for a Pokémon Sleep alternative

Credit first. Pokémon Sleep is the most popular sleep game on the App Store, with 4.5 stars from about 25,000 ratings in the US. It measures your night, sorts it into a sleep type (Dozing, Snoozing, Slumbering or Balanced), and the Pokémon that show up depend on that type and how long you slept. The official site says sleep is graded on two things: duration and consistency. The morning report also shows sleep stages, how long you took to fall asleep and whether you snored or talked.

The complaints people bring to a search like this usually fall into three groups:

  • The phone in the bed. Pokémon's own instructions say to keep the device plugged in, on the mattress, face down with the app running, and never under the pillow or blankets because it may overheat.
  • The spending. It is free to download, but the US listing shows a Premium Pass at $9.99 a month or $49.99 for six months, and diamond packs from $1.19 up to $97.99.
  • It only knows your sleep. Steps, workouts and heart data do not count. Some people want a creature that reacts to the whole day.

Before you switch: play Pokémon Sleep from your Apple Watch

If the mattress phone is the only problem, fix that and keep your Snorlax. Pokémon Sleep can read sleep your Apple Watch recorded, through Apple Health. Its pairing guide supports every Apple Watch except the first generation, Series 1 and Series 2.

  1. On iPhone, open the Health app, tap Get Started under Set Up Sleep, and follow the prompts for your sleep goal, bedtime and wake-up time. Turn on Track Sleep with Apple Watch. Apple notes the option appears during setup if your watch is already paired.
  2. In Pokémon Sleep, go to Settings and More, tap Pair a Device, then Pair Smartwatch, then Turn On All and Allow.
  3. Wear the watch to bed. Apple says it needs at least an hour of wear to record a night.
  4. In the morning, wait for the night to show up in Health, then tap Sync/Settings and Synchronize in the game.

The rules that trip people up, from Pokémon's guides: only nights recorded after you pair can sync, sleep entered or edited by hand in Health is ignored, and the night needs sleep stages and at least 90 minutes to register. The game does not run on the watch; your iPhone still does the playing. If a night comes up blank on the watch side, our guide on how to track sleep on Apple Watch covers the usual causes.

Nularo: a creature that judges the whole day

Nularo is the pick if the part you loved was the morning reveal, and the part you are tired of is the collecting. There is no Snorlax to feed and no dex to fill. A small creature reads Apple Health read-only (sleep, sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and blood oxygen), so with an Apple Watch on your wrist there is nothing to start at night and nothing to log.

Each morning a verdict notification arrives and the creature lands in one of four moods: glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious. The mood is set against your own 14-day baseline, not a fixed number of hours, so a good night for you counts as good. Around that sit Sleep, Stress and Energy scores, a running sleep debt (our sleep debt calculator shows the math for free), a Bedtime Pledge for tonight, evening Confessions, a Weekly Report Card and shareable roast cards. Creature Age, recalculated weekly, turns your sleep, bedtime consistency, steps, active minutes, resting heart rate and HRV into an age; it is a rules-based, for-fun number, not a medical one. Friend mode shows a friend only a mood word and a good-day count, never your health numbers.

The honest limits: nothing to collect, so if Pokémon were the motivation, stay with Pokémon Sleep. Nularo is iPhone only with no watch app, it works the morning after rather than in real time, and without a watch at night Apple Health has much thinner data to judge. It judges, it does not coach. It is a subscription with a paywall after onboarding: $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month.

Other sleep games worth a look

SleepTown is from Seekrtech, the studio behind Forest, and it does not measure your sleep at all. You set a bedtime and wake-up goal, start a building before bedtime, and shake the phone in the morning to see what you built. Miss your bedtime and you cannot build; use your phone during sleep time and the building is destroyed. Up to two days off a week keep a lie-in from ending your streak, and a circle lets family and friends build toward the same goals. It is $1.99 once, with 4.6 stars from about 1,500 ratings. One flag: the US listing's latest version is from November 2023. Good if your real problem is scrolling in bed.

Sleepagotchi is the closest in spirit to a collecting game: a Dino companion you check in with daily, plus an AI Sleep Coach. It reads Apple Watch, Fitbit, Oura, WHOOP and Mi Band data through Apple Health, or tracks with the phone. It is free to download, but the listing shows gem packs from $0.99 to $29.99 and special packs at $59.99, and an age rating of 9+ for loot boxes. Its 3.0 stars come from only 34 US ratings, so it is young.

Habitvs has a creature called Kova whose energy and mood shift with sleep, movement, HRV, recovery, mindfulness and daylight from Apple Health, plus a small daily promise, streaks and walking goals. It is free, and new enough that its US listing has no ratings yet. We compare it with other step and sleep pets in virtual pet apps that use Apple Health.

If you want a pet more than a sleep game

Some people search for Pokémon Sleep alternatives when what they really want is a cute companion. Finch is the big one: a self-care pet bird that grows as you finish small goals, with 4.9 stars from about 778,000 US ratings. It does not read Apple Health, so sleep is something you tick off yourself. Our Finch app review covers what is free and what Finch Plus adds, and self-care tamagotchi apps lists the rest.

And if you just want the number with no game, Apple's own Sleep app gives a free Sleep Score from 0 to 100 on watchOS 26 or later. Our guide on how to get a 100 sleep score on Apple Watch explains how it is built.

Which one to pick

  • You love the Pokémon, hate the phone on the bed: keep Pokémon Sleep and pair your Apple Watch.
  • You want the morning reveal without collecting, from your whole day: Nularo.
  • Your problem is the phone at midnight: SleepTown.
  • You want another collecting-style game with a coach: Sleepagotchi.
  • You want a free Apple Health creature: Habitvs.
  • You want a pet and do not care about tracking: Finch.

None of these is a medical device, and Pokémon says plainly that its app is for entertainment. If your sleep worries you, talk to a doctor.
