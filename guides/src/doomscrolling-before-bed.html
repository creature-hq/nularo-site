You said goodnight at 11. It is now 12:40, you are on your fourth comment thread about something that does not affect you, and your thumb keeps moving. So you search Reddit for how other people stopped, and find the same advice everyone finds: charge it in another room, go grayscale, set app limits, read a book. Here is why those fixes tend to last about a week, and how to set up the iPhone versions so they hold.

Why the Reddit fixes wear off

Read enough threads on doomscrolling in bed and two things stand out. The fixes are always the same handful. And the most common follow-up is some version of "that worked for a few days, then I found a way round it."

That is not a willpower story. It is a settings story. Apple's own documentation says Screen Time limits can be ignored by default once you reach them. App Limits put up a screen you can tap past. Downtime, without one specific toggle, is a reminder with an hourglass on it. A limit that you set, with a passcode that you know, is a limit you will negotiate with at 12:40 AM.

The fixes that do stick tend to share one feature: they add friction that cannot be undone from the bed. A charger across the room means getting up. A passcode someone else chose means asking them. A grey screen is less fun to look at, and it stays grey unless you go and change it.

It also helps to know you are in large company. In the American Academy of Sleep Medicine's 2025 Sleep Prioritization Survey of 2,007 US adults, half said they use a screen in bed every day (that counts TVs, laptops and e-readers too). 38% said using a phone or tablet before bed to read news, which the AASM calls doomscrolling, made their sleep slightly or significantly worse. Among 18 to 24 year olds it was 46%. Those are people describing their own nights, not sleep measurements, but they match what the threads say.

Make Screen Time actually block

This is the fix that most posts mention and most people set up half-way. The full version takes two minutes.

On iOS 26 and earlier, use Downtime. Open Settings, tap Screen Time, then tap Downtime and turn on Scheduled. Choose Every Day, or Customize Days if you want later weekends, and set the start and end times. Apple sends a reminder five minutes before Downtime begins. Now the part people skip: turn on Block at Downtime. Without it, you can keep using apps during Downtime. With it, restricted apps are dimmed with an hourglass and will not open. It needs a Screen Time passcode.

Let someone else choose the passcode. This is the single setting that turns Screen Time from a nudge into a lock. A partner, a housemate or a friend over FaceTime types four digits you do not see. If you share sleep goals with a friend already, our guide to sleep tracking with a partner or friends covers what each app lets them see.

On iOS 27, the same idea is called Screen Time Schedule. Open Settings, tap Screen Time, tap Screen Time Schedule and turn on Schedule. Tap Add Schedule, choose the days, tap Done, then edit the time windows. Each window can be renamed (call one "Bed") and given Time Allowances by category, such as Social Media or Entertainment. Apple says apps in a category stop working once they hit that category's allowance. Apple's guide also says Screen Time Schedules need iOS 27 or later on every Apple device you use, so an older iPad on the nightstand can leave a gap.

App Limits are the weaker tool. Settings, Screen Time, App Limits, Add Limit lets you cap a category or single apps per day. A daily cap does not care when you use it, though, so 40 minutes of Reddit at midnight looks the same to it as 40 minutes at lunch. For bedtime, a schedule beats a daily total.

Sleep Focus, Wind Down and grayscale

Screen Time decides what opens. These three decide how much the phone pulls at you before you even try.

Start Sleep Focus earlier with Wind Down. In the Health app, tap Search (Browse on some versions), tap Sleep, then Full Schedule & Options. Under Additional Details, tap Wind Down. Apple lets you set it anywhere from 15 minutes to 3 hours before your bedtime, and Sleep Focus begins when Wind Down starts. That moves the quiet phone forward from "lights out" to "time to start getting ready." Our sleepmaxxing apps guide walks through the rest of Apple's schedule screen.

Trim what Sleep Focus lets through. Go to Settings, Focus, Sleep. Choose which people and apps can still notify you at night, and silence the rest. Tap Options to have the Lock Screen dim while Sleep Focus is on. Fewer notifications lighting up the screen means fewer reasons to pick it up. The Sleep Focus screen also has Shortcuts: tap Edit and swap the default ones for a book app or a podcast, so the first thing you see is the replacement habit, not the feed.

Put grayscale one click away. Go to Settings, Accessibility, Display & Text Size, Color Filters, turn it on and choose Grayscale. Then go to Settings, Accessibility, Accessibility Shortcut and select Color Filters. Now a triple-click of the side button (or Home button) turns the screen grey and back. Grey feeds are noticeably duller. The honest catch is the same as with every Reddit fix: a triple-click turns it back off, so pair it with the Screen Time block rather than relying on it alone.

If the reason your phone is in bed with you is the alarm, the wake-up alarm in an Apple Watch sleep schedule goes off on the watch, and Apple's alarm options let you choose whether it also plays on iPhone. That lets the phone charge across the room.

What your Apple Watch sees, and what it misses

Here is something none of the doomscrolling articles mention: your watch cannot tell that you were scrolling. Apple's developer documentation says the watch only records Awake time that falls between two stretches of sleep. Lying in bed on your phone before you drift off is not drawn at all. And since watchOS 11 and iOS 18, Apple no longer records In Bed time, the number that used to show the gap; there is more on that change in why Apple Watch time in bed is missing.

What the watch does see is the result. You fell asleep at 1:10 instead of 11:20, so:

  • Your Sleep Score drops. On watchOS 26 and later, 30 of the 100 points are bedtime consistency, based on when you fell asleep over the last 13 nights. A run of late scrolls keeps costing points for about two weeks. The full scoring is in apps that rate your sleep.
  • Your duration shrinks. The alarm does not move just because you did, so the scroll comes straight out of Time Asleep. Several of those in a row is sleep debt, which Apple does not total for you.
  • Your bedtime drifts on the chart. In Health, open Sleep and tap W at the top of the chart. Late nights stand out as bars that start later. Sleep consistency apps compares how other apps grade that drift.

To see the scrolling side, open Settings, tap Screen Time, then See All App & Website Activity, and switch between Day and Week. Further down you will find Pickups, a count of how often you picked the phone up. Put that next to the week's sleep chart and the pattern is usually obvious without any app at all.

None of this is a diagnosis. If you lie awake whether or not the phone is there, or you are exhausted however early you put it down, that is one for a doctor rather than a setting.
