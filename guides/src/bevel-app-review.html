How Bevel works: the scores and where they come from

Bevel does not have its own sensor. It reads what your Apple Watch writes to Apple Health and turns it into four daily scores plus a battery.

  • Sleep Score (out of 100%) uses time asleep against your personal sleep need, REM and deep sleep, heart rate dip, sleep efficiency and sleep continuity. It also tracks a Sleep Bank: your last seven days of sleep, naps included, against your sleep goal.
  • Recovery Score (1% to 100%) combines your Sleep Score with resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate, blood oxygen and wrist temperature.
  • Strain Score measures how hard your body worked, adjusted for your sleep and recovery.
  • Stress Score runs through the day from heart rate and HRV. Our comparison of free stress apps for Apple Watch sets it next to Athlytic's and Welltory's.
  • Energy Bank combines all four into a gauge that fills and drains through the day.

Three details the other reviews skip, all from Bevel's help center. First, it labels each reading "above normal", "normal" or "below normal" against your own 60-day baseline, and it says calibration takes 2 to 6 weeks. Judge it after a month, not after a weekend. Second, it measures HRV with RMSSD by default, while Apple Health has traditionally used SDNN, so Bevel's HRV will not match the Health app (Bevel notes that Series 12 and Ultra 4 now record RMSSD natively). Third, it takes resting heart rate from your sleep, not Apple's daily value. If you have wondered whether Apple's resting heart rate includes sleep, this is why the two numbers differ.

The catch: no overnight data, no scores. Bevel says that without resting heart rate and HRV from sleep it cannot produce a Sleep Score, Recovery Score or Target Strain. If you charge your watch at night, fix that first (our guide on how to track sleep on Apple Watch covers the setup).

Price: $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year in the US. The App Store also lists Bevel Intelligence credit packs from $4.99 (350 credits) to $49.99 (4,200 credits), so the AI coach can be topped up without Pro. Things to know before you tap:

  • The trial varies. Bevel's FAQ says trial availability and length may vary; check the upgrade screen.
  • Old annual prices are kept. Annual subscribers keep their original price while the plan stays active, with a 60-day grace period if it lapses.
  • Family Sharing works on Pro, at no extra cost.
  • Version 3.3 moved weather into Pro. The new Home dashboard has weather backgrounds, and the release notes say weather data is now part of Bevel Pro. Bevel promised not to move existing free features behind the paywall, so expect some debate about this one.

To cancel on iPhone, open Settings, tap your name, tap Subscriptions, tap Bevel, then Cancel Subscription. If only one or two Pro features tempt you, our guide to free Bevel alternatives lists a free stand-in for each.

If you want the verdict without the dashboard: Nularo

Bevel's strength is depth: dozens of numbers and an AI to explain them. Some people open it every morning and still cannot say whether last night was good. Nularo, our app, is built for them. It is an iPhone app with a creature that reads Apple Health read-only: sleep and sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and blood oxygen. Like Bevel, you log nothing.

Each morning you get a verdict notification, and the creature lands in one of four moods (glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious) set against your own 14-day baseline. Behind it sit Sleep, Stress and Energy scores, a running sleep debt, and a weekly Creature Age from your sleep, bedtime consistency, steps, active minutes, resting heart rate and HRV, with a per-habit breakdown. Creature Age is a rules-based, for-fun number built from public-health guidelines, not a biological age test like the one in Bevel Pro.

The honest limits. Nularo judges, it does not coach: no AI chat, no strain gauge, no food log, no training plans. It is a morning-after read, not real time. It is iPhone only and a subscription with a hard paywall after onboarding: $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month, with no free tier like Bevel's. Without an Apple Watch its data is thin, same as Bevel's.

What Bevel does well, and where it falls short

The good. The free plan is the real product, not a demo, which is rare in this category. Its daily scores cover what a Whoop alternative for Apple Watch needs to without a second wearable. Food logging, strength training and recovery live in one app. It is updated often (3.3 added workout notes, smarter merging of data from more than one device and a better food search), and it is available in 14 languages.

The trade-offs.

  • It is a lot. Four scores, a battery, monitors, a timeline and an AI coach. If you want one answer per morning, it can feel like homework.
  • The free AI is a taste. Free users get limited messages and 90 days of history; the coach is the main reason Pro exists.
  • The paywall keeps growing at the edges. Pro's price went up with Bevel 3.0 in April 2026, and 3.3 added weather to it. The core scores are still free.
  • Numbers disagree with Apple. That is by design, but if you check both, Apple's free sleep score (in the Sleep app on the watch, built from duration, bedtime consistency and interruptions) will rarely match Bevel's. Our page on whether Apple Watch has a recovery score explains what Apple now shows for free.

Bevel's own listing says it is not a medical device. The same goes for every app on this page: if a number worries you, talk to a doctor.

Who should use Bevel, and who should not

  • Pick Bevel if you train, wear your watch to bed and want free recovery, strain and sleep scores in one place. Start free and live with it for a month.
  • Pay for Pro only if you will actually use the AI coach with your full history, Biological Age or Health Records. For an age number alone, compare the options in biological age apps for Apple Watch.
  • Look elsewhere if dashboards stress you out or you mainly want a nudge about sleep. Our list of apps that rate your sleep covers simpler options.
