Why the search results feel off

Search "best gamified fitness apps" and a good share of what comes back is written by companies selling a gamified fitness app, with their own product at number one. Other lists are a few years old and still quote prices that changed long ago. That is why people go looking for threads instead.

This guide does it the boring way. Every app below was checked against its current App Store listing or its developer's own help pages in November 2026. Prices are only stated where the developer states them. And the apps are sorted by the kind of game they are, because "gamified" covers very different things: a story you run through, a creature you collect, a spaceship that eats your steps, or a to-do list with hit points. The one that works for you is the one whose game you would play anyway.

The free game already on your iPhone

Before downloading anything, look at the Fitness app. It already has the two mechanics most gamified apps are built on, rewards and rivals, and most lists skip it because nobody is selling it.

Awards. Open Fitness, tap Summary, then tap the Awards area. You earn awards for streaks, personal records and milestones, and the list shows the ones you have not earned yet, which is where the pull comes from.

Competitions. This is the part people underrate. In Fitness, tap Sharing, then add a friend with Invite a Friend (first time, tap Get Started). When they accept, tap their name, tap Compete, then invite them. Apple's rules are simple: the competition lasts 7 days, you get a point for every percent of your Activity rings you close, up to 600 points a day and 4,200 for the week, and the watch tells you during the week whether you are ahead or behind. Most at the end wins.

The catch: without an Apple Watch, Fitness only has a Move ring, estimated from your iPhone's motion sensors. The Exercise and Stand rings need a watch. A story: Zombies, Run!

If you get bored on runs, this is the one to try first. You are Runner 5. Through your headphones a story plays between the songs on your own playlist: rescue survivors, collect supplies, outrun the horde. Each run is a mission, and the supplies you pick up go toward rebuilding a town called Abel Township between runs. The listing counts 500+ missions across 11 seasons, co-created by the novelist Naomi Alderman, and a Couch to 5K program told as a story.

It is not only for runners. The developer says it works at any pace, on a walk, a treadmill or in a wheelchair. It tracks by GPS and gives you the usual stats.

The money: you can try it for free, and a subscription unlocks every mission. The price has changed several times over the years, so check it in the app rather than trusting an old list. Background GPS costs battery, which the listing itself warns about.

Collecting while you walk: Pikmin Bloom, Pokémon GO, Walkr

These turn the walk you were going to take anyway into progress in a game. They suit people who hate the word "workout".

Pikmin Bloom. Walking grows seedlings into Pikmin (150+ Decor Pikmin to collect) and plants flowers along your route on the map. Weekly Challenges let you team up with friends anywhere toward a shared step goal. With your permission it reads Apple Health, so walking still counts when the app is closed. Free to play with in-game purchases, iPhone only (no iPad).

Pokémon GO. The famous one. The fitness hook is Adventure Sync: turn it on from the main menu under Settings, then General, connect Apple Health, and set location to always. Distance you walk while the app is closed then counts toward hatching eggs and your buddy Pokémon, and you get a weekly report plus bonus items for hitting weekly goals. Free with in-game purchases. If you like Pokémon but your real problem is sleep, gamified sleep tracker apps covers Pokémon Sleep.

Walkr. Your steps become fuel for a small spaceship, and fuel takes you to 100+ planets with 30+ missions. Friends can join as co-pilots or a fleet, and there are leagues to compete in. It comes from SPARKFUL, the makers of Plant Nanny. The download is free; an optional SPARKFUL subscription is $7.99 a month after a 7-day trial and covers all their apps.

Quests for your to-do list: Habitica

Habitica is not a fitness tracker. It is a habit and to-do app dressed as a role-playing game, which is why it shows up on so many fitness lists: you decide what counts ("20 squats", "walk at lunch"), and your avatar earns gold, experience and items when you tick it off. There are streak counters, levels, and gear and pets to collect.

The strongest part is the party. During a boss quest, a Daily you miss damages everyone in your party, not just you, so a friend has a reason to nag. It is free, with optional purchases. The weakness is the same as its strength: nothing is measured. If you tick it off, it happened. Apps that read Apple Health cannot be fooled that easily, and cannot be forgotten either, which matters once the novelty wears off. Where Nularo fits

Full disclosure: Nularo is our app, and it is a different kind of game. There is no map, loot or story. There is a creature, and it lives on your Apple Health data.

Nularo reads Apple Health read-only (sleep, sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV and more), so you never log anything. Each morning a verdict notification arrives and the creature is in one of four moods: glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious. The mood is set against your own 14-day baseline, so a good day for you counts as good. Creature Age, recalculated weekly, turns your sleep, steps (8,000 a day), active minutes (30 a day), resting heart rate and HRV into an age, with a breakdown of which habit added the years. It is a rules-based, for-fun number, not a medical one. There is also a Weekly Report Card and shareable roast cards, if being mocked works on you (more in apps that roast you).

The honest limitation: it judges, it does not coach. It will not plan a run or tell a story while you are out. It is iPhone only, it works the morning after rather than in real time, and without an Apple Watch the sleep and heart data is thin. It is a subscription after onboarding: $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month. Frequently asked questions

What is the best free gamified fitness app?

The one already on your iPhone. Apple's Fitness app has awards and 7-day competitions with friends at no cost. Among third-party apps, Pikmin Bloom and Pokémon GO are free to play with optional in-game purchases, and Habitica is free with optional purchases.

Is Zombies, Run! free?

You can try it for free, and its App Store listing says a subscription unlocks every mission. Check the current price in the app before you subscribe, because subscriptions renew automatically.

Do Pikmin Bloom and Pokémon GO count steps when the app is closed?

Yes, with your permission. Both App Store listings say they use Apple Health to credit walking distance while the app is closed. In Pokémon GO the feature is called Adventure Sync, and its help center says it needs location permission set to always and a connection to Apple Health.

Do Apple Fitness competitions need an Apple Watch?

Competitions are scored on your Activity rings. Without a watch, the Fitness app only shows the Move ring, estimated from the iPhone's motion sensors, so a watch makes the competition fairer and the scoring fuller.

Is Nularo a fitness game?

Not exactly. There is no story, map or loot. A creature reads your Apple Health data each night and lands in one of four moods against your own 14-day baseline. Steps and active minutes count, and so does sleep. It is a subscription: $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month.
