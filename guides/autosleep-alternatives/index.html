You opened AutoSleep this morning to see how you slept and got four rings, a sleep bank, Sleep Fuel, readiness and a graph you had to squint at. Meanwhile your watch already shows a Sleep Score for free. You want to know if something simpler, or something different, is worth the switch.

First: what AutoSleep does that is hard to replace

Credit first, because AutoSleep is very good at what it does. It costs $9.99 once, and its US App Store listing says "No extra In App Purchases. No subscriptions" and no data upload. It holds 4.7 stars from about 62,000 ratings, lists App Store Best of awards for every year from 2019 to 2025, and was last updated on November 3, 2026. It needs an Apple Watch Series 4 or later on watchOS 9 or later.

The feature list is long: sleep rings for duration, quality, deep sleep and heart rate, a sleep bank, a Sleep Rating, readiness, overnight blood oxygen, respiration rate, wrist temperature, live tracking during the night, a smart alarm on the watch, and an estimate of time in bed even on nights you charge the watch. Since its latest update it also has a clear switch between its own tracking and Apple Sleep App Mode, where it reads the nights Apple's Sleep app recorded. If you love numbers, nothing on this list beats it for one payment.

People who leave usually hit one of five walls:

  • It is too dense. You wanted one answer, not a dashboard.
  • Apple caught up for the basics. Stages and a score now come free on the watch.
  • No recordings. AutoSleep's snoring view needs a separate app (AutoSnore).
  • No watch at night. You charge it overnight and want a phone tracker.
  • No plan. It measures, it does not tell you what tonight should look like.

If AutoSleep was just too much

Apple's Sleep app is the obvious first stop, and it may be all you need. On the watch, open Settings, tap Sleep and turn on Sleep Tracking (or on iPhone, open the Watch app, tap My Watch, then Sleep). Apple says the watch then adds your sleep to Health. Each morning the Sleep app shows Awake, REM, Core and Deep sleep for the last 14 days, and on watchOS 26 or later a Sleep Score built from duration (50 points), bedtime consistency (30) and interruptions (20). Charge to at least 30% before bed and wear the watch for at least an hour of sleep. Our walkthrough of how to track sleep on Apple Watch covers the setup, and Apple Sleep Score vs AutoSleep compares the two grades line by line.

If you want recordings, or the watch stays on the charger

Pillow (4.4 stars, about 96,000 ratings) tracks automatically when you wear the watch to bed, or with an iPhone or iPad, and wakes you at the lightest sleep stage it can find. It shows last night's report and heart rate on the watch, and Premium adds sound recordings, unlimited history, sleep notes and nap modes. Premium is $39.99 a year or $19.99 a month. If you came to AutoSleep for the smart alarm, this is the most direct swap with recordings built in.

Sleep Cycle (4.7 stars, about 33,000 ratings) needs no wearable at all: put the phone on the nightstand and it tracks with sound or motion, detects snoring, coughing and sleep talking, and runs a smart alarm. Apple Health sync and data export are Premium features, and Premium is sold at several prices on the App Store, from $2.99 to $57.99. How to switch without breaking your Health data

AutoSleep writes its sleep to Apple Health, so a careless switch leaves you with two versions of every night. Four steps fix it:

  1. Export first. AutoSleep's listing includes export options to save your data. Its rings and ratings live in the app, not in Health, so export before you delete it if you want them.
  2. Turn on Apple's tracking. If AutoSleep was doing its own tracking, Apple's may be off. On the watch, Settings, Sleep, Sleep Tracking.
  3. Set the source order. On iPhone, open Health, tap Browse, tap Sleep, scroll to Data Sources & Access and tap Edit. Drag the source you want to the top. To stop using AutoSleep's data, tap the checkmark next to it. Tap Done. Apple says the top source takes priority, and that new apps are listed at the top automatically, so check again after installing anything.
  4. Reset your expectations. Every app draws the line between awake and asleep differently, so the new totals will not match AutoSleep's. Follow the new app's own trend for a few weeks. Our guide to apps that rate your sleep explains why the scores never agree.

Not sure you want to leave at all? You can keep AutoSleep in Apple Sleep App Mode, let Apple's app do the tracking, and add one app on top for what AutoSleep lacks. Frequently asked questions

Is there a free alternative to AutoSleep?

Yes. Apple's own Sleep app is free on Apple Watch and records Awake, REM, Core and Deep sleep, with a 0 to 100 Sleep Score on watchOS 26 or later. Sleep++ is also free and tracks automatically when you wear the watch to bed, and Bevel's sleep and recovery scores are free.

Do I still need AutoSleep now that Apple Watch has a Sleep Score?

Not if you only read the score. Apple's score covers duration, bedtime consistency and interruptions for free. AutoSleep still earns its $9.99 if you want heart rate in your grade, a sleep bank, readiness, live tracking or a smart alarm on the watch.

Will I lose my AutoSleep history if I switch?

AutoSleep writes its sleep to Apple Health, and its listing includes export options to save your data. Export from AutoSleep before you delete it if you want its own ratings and rings, since other apps only see the raw sleep entries in Health.

Why does my new app show different sleep totals than AutoSleep?

Each app decides when you fell asleep and woke up with its own method, so the same night can differ by half an hour or more. Pick one app and follow its trend over weeks. None of these apps is a medical device.

Is Nularo an AutoSleep alternative?

Only for the morning part. Nularo does not track on the watch. It reads the sleep Apple's Sleep app or another tracker saved to Apple Health and sends a morning verdict, a Sleep score, sleep debt and a creature mood against your own 14-day baseline. It is iPhone only, $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial or $9.99 a month.
