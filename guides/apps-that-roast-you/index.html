Short answer

Free option first: screenshot your own data (Settings > Screen Time > See All App & Website Activity, or Health > Browse > Sleep) and ask any chatbot that accepts images to roast it. Dedicated apps split by target: photo roast apps like Roast Me and ClapBack AI roast your face or a prompt, Cleo has a Roast Mode for your spending, CARROT Weather and CARROT Fit have snarky personalities, and Nularo roasts last night's sleep from Apple Health every morning.

Encouraging apps have stopped working on you. The confetti, the "you've got this", the badge for drinking one glass of water. You want something that looks at what you actually did and says, out loud, that it was not great.

The free way: roast your own screenshot

Before downloading anything, know that the roast is the easy part. Any chatbot that accepts images can roast you if you hand it material. The material is what makes it funny, and your iPhone already collects plenty.

  • Your screen time. Open Settings, tap Screen Time, then tap See All App & Website Activity. Switch between Week and Day at the top. If the report is empty, App & Website Activity is turned off; Apple's Screen Time setup guide shows how to turn it on.
  • Your sleep. Open the Health app, tap Browse, then tap Sleep. If you wear an Apple Watch to bed, you get time asleep and stages. Without a watch, you mostly get time in bed from your sleep schedule.
  • Your steps. Health app, Browse, Activity, Steps. Tap the W or M view for a week or a month of evidence.

Screenshot one of those, paste it into a chatbot and type "roast me". It works, and it costs nothing. The catch is that it is a one-off. It only knows the screenshot you gave it, it does not remember last week, and you have to remember to do it. The apps below exist to make the roast automatic, or to roast something your phone does not measure.

A small privacy habit worth keeping: crop out names, notification previews and anything else you would not post publicly before you upload a screenshot anywhere.

Photo and text roast apps

These are the apps most people find first. The pattern is the same across them: pick a photo or type something about yourself, choose a style, get a few lines of AI-written insults. Roast Me describes "customized, comedic takedowns" from a photo or prompt. ClapBack AI lets you trade roasts with AI characters. Roast Royale returns three short roasts per photo, built for sharing. Roast Characters lets you pick a character, an intensity and a language. Most are free to download and then meter you with credits or a subscription.

They are good for one thing: a quick laugh in a group chat. They are weak at the thing you probably came for, because a roast based only on your face is a roast about your face. Nothing in it is about your choices, so it cannot change anything and gets repetitive fast.

The bigger question is the photo. At least one listing in this category says it sends your image off for vision analysis, which is how these apps work. Before uploading a face, yours or a friend's, scroll down the App Store listing to App Privacy and see what the developer says it collects and whether it is linked to you. If a roast app is vague about it, pick another one. There are dozens.

Apps that roast what you actually did

CARROT Weather is the most polished app in this whole genre, and it is a weather app. Its forecasts come with commentary from a sarcastic robot, and you choose how mean it is: professional (no jokes), friendly, snarky, homicidal or overkill, with a separate profanity filter for the crude end. You can also switch the personality off. It is free to download, with Premium subscription tiers for extra data sources, alerts and widgets. It roasts you for going outside without checking, which is a narrow kind of roast, but it is very good at it.

CARROT Fit, from the same developer, is where roast apps got their reputation. It launched in 2014 as a weight-loss app whose AI berated users for gaining weight, and eating disorder campaigners asked Apple to remove it. Today its App Store listing describes a snarky 7-minute workout app, and the listing showed no update since 2021, so treat it as a curiosity. The same developer also makes CARROT To-Do, a task list with a personality.

Cleo is an AI money app, and its App Store listing describes a Roast Mode in which it "gives it to you straight" about where your money went, plus a gentler Hype Mode. It is free to download, with paid plans for features like cash advances. The roast is the marketing hook; the app underneath is a budgeting and banking product, so judge it on that before linking an account.

When a roast stops being funny

The CARROT Fit story is worth remembering when you pick one of these. There is a real difference between an app that roasts a choice (three hours on TikTok, a 2 a.m. bedtime, a $40 delivery order) and one that roasts your body. The first is about something you did yesterday and can do differently tonight. The second is about you.

A few questions sort the good ones from the bad:

  • Does it roast behavior, or appearance and weight?
  • Can you turn the intensity down, or off, on a bad day?
  • Is it based on real data, or does it guess?
  • Does it say what happens to your photos and data?

And if a roast about sleep or eating lands somewhere it should not, close the app. If the thing being roasted is a real worry, that is one for a doctor, not an app.

The app that roasts your sleep: Nularo

Full disclosure: Nularo is our app, and it is the reason this page exists. When we searched for an app that roasts you for bad sleep, we found the photo apps above and nothing that looked at what you actually did last night.

Nularo reads Apple Health read-only: sleep and sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and SpO2. You never log anything. Each morning it sends a verdict notification about last night, and a creature sits in one of four moods (glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious) measured against your own 14-day baseline. So the roast is about you compared with you, not you compared with a stranger's 8 hours. If you slept five hours, it already knows, and it will mention it.

The good roasts can be shared as roast cards. There is a 7-day sleep debt figure, a Bedtime Pledge for tonight and evening Confessions if you want to get ahead of the verdict. A Weekly Report Card grades the week, and a weekly Creature Age turns your habits into an age, with a breakdown of which habit added the years. It is a rules-based, for-fun number built from public-health guidelines, compared with other apps in biological age apps for Apple Watch. If you want a friend to see you get roasted, friend mode shows them only a mood word and a good-day count, never your health numbers (more in sleep tracking with a partner or friends).

It never roasts your body or weight, and it does not give advice. It judges; it does not coach.

The honest limitations. It is iPhone only. It only knows what Apple Health records, so without an Apple Watch at night the data is thinner and the roasts are vaguer. It is morning-after, not real time, so it will not stop you scrolling at 1 a.m. (SleepTown, in our gamified sleep tracker roundup, is better at that). Frequently asked questions

Is there an app that roasts you for free?

Most photo roast apps are free to download and then limit you with credits or a subscription. The truly free route is a general chatbot: paste in a photo or a screenshot of your Screen Time report and ask it to roast you. It only knows what is in the screenshot.

Is there an app that roasts your spending?

Cleo, an AI money app, describes a Roast Mode in its App Store listing that comments bluntly on where your money went. It is free to download, with paid plans for its extra features. Check what your plan includes in the app before you pay.

Is it safe to upload my photo to a roast app?

It depends on the app. Some listings say your image is sent to a server for analysis. Read the App Store privacy label (scroll down on the listing to App Privacy) before you upload a face, and skip any app that does not say what it does with the photo.

What happened to CARROT Fit?

CARROT Fit launched in 2014 as a weight-loss app with a famously mean AI and drew complaints from eating disorder campaigners. Today its App Store listing describes a snarky 7-minute workout app from the same developer as CARROT Weather, and the listing showed no update since 2021.

Does Nularo roast you?
