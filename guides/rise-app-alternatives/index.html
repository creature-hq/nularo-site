What Rise does, and why people look elsewhere

Credit first. Rise, from Rise Science, has 4.7 stars from about 75,000 ratings on the US App Store and was updated on November 22, 2026. Its core idea is sleep debt: the gap between the sleep you need and the sleep you got. Rise's own explainer says it rolls that gap over the last 14 nights, with last night counting for 15% and the 13 nights before it for the other 85%, recent nights weighted more. It compares that with a personal sleep need it estimates for you, rather than a flat 8 hours. On top sit energy levels through the day, a melatonin window, a smart alarm, habit reminders, sleep sounds and calendar integration. It reads sleep from Apple Health, Apple Watch, Fitbit, Oura and phone trackers such as Sleep Cycle and ShutEye.

People who leave usually hit one of three walls:

  • The price. The listing shows 1 week free, then $69.99 a year, and says a paid subscription or free trial is required to access the content. There is no free tier to fall back on.
  • The number disagrees with the watch. Rise uses its own sleep need and window, so its debt rarely matches what your Apple Watch or another app says.
  • Too much coaching. Some people only wanted the debt and a bedtime, not an energy schedule.

Start with what your iPhone already does

Apple's Health app does not show a sleep debt figure, but it holds everything you need to work one out. If Sleep is not set up yet, open Health, tap Get Started under Set Up Sleep, then set your Sleep Goal, Bedtime and Wake Up, Sleep Focus and Track Sleep with Apple Watch. Apple says you need to wear the watch for at least an hour a night for data to arrive.

To read your nights, tap Browse, then Sleep, then Sleep again, and switch to W for the week. Tap Show More Sleep Data and the Amounts view lists Time Asleep next to your Sleep Goal. Subtract the week's Time Asleep from seven times your goal and you have a 7-night debt. Our guide to whether Apple Watch tracks sleep debt walks through it night by night, and the free sleep debt calculator does the sum for you if you type the nights in, including a 14-night mode to compare with Rise.

What you do not get: an estimated personal need, an energy curve or a reminder that tonight matters more than usual. That is what the apps below add.

Sleep Ledger is the closest thing to Rise's main screen, stripped down. Its listing promises "two things: a number (your sleep debt) and a plan (when to go to bed tonight)". The debt balance, tonight's bedtime recommendation, a 0 to 100 Sleep Score, a sleep diary, Lock Screen widgets and Apple Watch complications are free. Premium adds the full day-by-day recovery plan, trend history, debt weighted by sleep stages and CSV export, at $4.99 a month, $29.99 a year (with a 2-week trial) or $79.99 lifetime. It processes data on the device and needs no account. It is a new app with only a handful of ratings so far, so expect rough edges.

Athlytic (MyndArc) puts sleep debt inside a recovery app. Its sleep section lists debt next to stages, consistency, latency and efficiency, and a Target Sleep card gives a nightly goal and bedtime built from that day's recovery, exertion and debt. What you lose when you leave Rise

  • A personal sleep need. Rise estimates yours. The others compare with a goal you set, so pick an honest one.
  • The melatonin window and energy schedule. No app here copies them exactly. Athlytic's Battery and Bevel's Energy Bank are the closest, and they work from heart data, not a circadian model.
  • Non-Apple sources. Rise reads Fitbit and Oura directly. These alternatives read Apple Health, so another device only counts if it writes its sleep there.
  • Your subscription, if you forget it. To stop Rise renewing, open Settings, tap your name, tap Subscriptions, choose Rise and tap the cancel option (you may need to scroll). If there is no cancel button, Apple says it is already canceled.

Expect a different number after you switch. Same nights, different window and target, different total. Pick one app and follow its trend rather than comparing apps day by day. None of these apps is a medical device; if your sleep worries you, talk to a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free alternative to Rise?

Sleep Ledger is the closest. Its listing says today's debt balance, tonight's bedtime recommendation, a Sleep Score and Apple Watch complications are always free, with a premium tier for the full recovery plan and history. Apple's Health app is free too, but it shows Time Asleep against your Sleep Goal, not a debt total, so you add it up yourself.

How much does Rise cost?

On the US App Store in November 2026, Rise offers a 1-week free trial and then $69.99 a year for Sleep & Energy Premium. Its listing also shows other Sleep Improvement Membership prices from $9.99 to $69.99, depending on the offer. The listing says a paid subscription or free trial is required to access the app's content.

Why does my sleep debt differ between Rise and other apps?

Each app uses its own target and window. Rise rolls the gap over 14 nights, weights last night at 15%, and compares it with a sleep need it estimates for you. Most other apps compare with a fixed goal you set, so the same Apple Health nights produce a different total.

Does Rise work with Apple Watch?

Yes. Its listing says it reads sleep through Apple Health and Apple Watch, plus Fitbit, Oura and phone trackers such as Sleep Cycle and ShutEye, and it has a watch app (watchOS 9.1 or later). Every alternative here also reads Apple Watch sleep from Apple Health.

Is Nularo a replacement for Rise?

For the sleep debt and the nudge to get to bed, yes. For Rise's energy curve, melatonin window and coaching, no: Nularo judges your night, it does not coach your day. It is iPhone only and costs $39.99 a year after a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month.
