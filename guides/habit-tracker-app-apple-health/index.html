You set up a habit tracker on January 2nd with eight habits and a nice color for each. By week three you were ticking "10,000 steps" at 11 PM from memory, and by week five the app was in a folder. The habits were not the problem. Logging them was.

What your iPhone already tracks without a single tap

Before you download anything, check which of your habits Apple already counts. If it is movement or sleep, the free answer may be enough.

Movement: the Fitness app. Carry your iPhone and its motion sensors track your steps, distance and flights climbed to estimate active calories, which fill the red Move ring. No Apple Watch needed. With an Apple Watch you also get the green Exercise ring and the blue Stand ring. In Fitness, tap Summary, then the Awards area to see awards for streaks, personal records and milestones. Tap the Trends area to compare your last 90 days with the last 365 (trends start after 180 days of activity).

Sleep: the Health app. Open Health, tap Browse, tap Sleep, then Get Started under Set Up Sleep to set a sleep goal, bedtime and wake time. With an Apple Watch worn to bed, every night is recorded against that goal. Our step-by-step for tracking sleep on Apple Watch covers the setup.

What Apple does not give you is a checklist of your own habits with a streak for each one. That is the gap a habit tracker app fills, and the useful split is simple: does it read Apple Health, or do you tick everything yourself?

Habit trackers that fill themselves in

Streaks. The best-established auto tracker on iPhone and an Apple Design Award winner. You choose up to 24 habits, and with your permission Streaks completes them from more than 80 types of Apple Health data, including steps, workouts, sleep, water, mindful minutes and calories. It also has Screen Time habits for cutting back on your phone, negative habits for quitting things, and a full Apple Watch app. You can share a habit with a friend who also uses Streaks so they can keep you accountable. It holds 4.8 stars from about 27,000 ratings and costs $5.99 once, with no subscription. The catch: it is a plain list, so if a checklist is what bored you, it may bore you again.

Nularo. This is the pick if your habits are sleep and movement and you never want to open a checklist again. There is no list at all. Nularo reads Apple Health read-only (sleep and sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and blood oxygen). Each morning a verdict notification arrives and a small creature is in one of four moods: glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious, measured against your own 14-day baseline. Instead of streaks there is Creature Age, recalculated weekly from sleep duration, bedtime consistency, steps (8,000 a day), active minutes (30 a day), resting heart rate and HRV, with a breakdown of which habit added the years. It is a rules-based, for-fun number, not a medical one. A Bedtime Pledge, sleep debt and a Weekly Report Card round it out.

The honest limits. Nularo is not a general habit tracker: you cannot add "read 20 pages", and anything Apple Health does not record is invisible to it. Without an Apple Watch the sleep and heart data is thin. It is iPhone only, works the morning after rather than in real time, and judges rather than coaches. It is a subscription with a hard paywall after onboarding: $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month. Many people pair it with a manual tracker for the habits Health cannot see.

Habitify. A polished tracker with charts, streaks and milestones that logs progress from Apple Health, Google Health, Fitbit, Strava, Screen Time and RescueTime. Read the pricing closely: the download is free, but the App Store listing puts automated Apple Health integrations in Pro, at $8.99 a month or $49.99 a year (the cheaper Plus plan does not include them). It runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch, with 4.6 stars from about 7,200 ratings.

Habit Tracker by InnerGrow. The cheap way in, with 4.8 stars from about 149,000 ratings. Its listing says it reads data for some habits from Apple Health, naming steps, energy, walking and running, and lists water, sleep, exercise and calories under its Health sync. Free to download, with Premium at $4.99 a month or a $19.99 lifetime option.

When a manual tracker is the better call

Apple Health cannot see most of the habits people actually try to build: reading, flossing, journaling, no sugar, calling your mum. For those, auto-tracking adds nothing, and a fast manual tracker wins.

HabitKit is the tidy one: a tile grid per habit, widgets that log without opening the app, streaks and stats, no account and no ads, with data kept on the device. 4.8 stars from about 2,600 ratings, free with an optional Pro. Its listing does not mention Apple Health.

Atoms calls itself the official Atomic Habits app: a tracker plus bite-sized lessons built on James Clear's book, with an Apple Watch app for logging on the move. 4.8 stars from about 11,000 ratings; free, with Pro at $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year.

(Not Boring) Habits is for people streaks stress out. Its listing says it plainly: no streaks, no guilt. The goal is 60 repetitions of a habit, and a missed day costs nothing. Free, with Plus at $14.99 a year. Productive (4.6 stars, about 91,000 ratings) is another popular manual option with a premium subscription.

Which one to pick

  • Your habits are steps and workouts, and you want it free: the Fitness app's rings and awards.
  • You want a proper habit list that mostly ticks itself, no subscription: Streaks.
  • You also use Strava or Fitbit and like charts: Habitify Pro.
  • Your habits are sleep and movement and you are done with checklists: Nularo, or the free pets in virtual pet apps that use Apple Health.
  • Your habits are reading, flossing and journaling: HabitKit or Atoms.
  • You need someone watching: Streaks' shared habits, or the free options in free accountability buddy apps.

One habit worth special treatment is bedtime, because a tracker you tick at midnight cannot tell you went to bed at 1:40. Sleep consistency apps measure that from your watch, and revenge bedtime procrastination explains why it is the habit people break most.

Frequently asked questions

Which habit tracker app works with Apple Health?

Streaks completes habits from more than 80 types of Apple Health data and costs $5.99 once. Habitify, Habit Tracker by InnerGrow and Nularo also read Apple Health. HabitKit, Atoms, Productive and (Not Boring) Habits do not mention Apple Health in their App Store listings, so you tick those yourself.

Is there a free habit tracker that tracks automatically?

The Fitness app on iPhone is free and fills its Move ring from your iPhone's steps without any logging, with awards for streaks. Among third-party apps, Habit Tracker by InnerGrow is free to download and reads steps and some other data from Apple Health, with an optional Premium. Habitify's automatic Apple Health logging needs Pro.

Does Apple have a built-in habit tracker?

Not a general one. The closest built-in tools are the Fitness app's Activity rings and awards, and the sleep goal and sleep schedule in the Health app. They track movement and sleep automatically, but you cannot add your own habit like reading.

Can a habit tracker track reading or meditation automatically?

Only if something writes that data to Apple Health. Meditation apps that save mindful minutes to Health can complete a mindful-minutes habit in Streaks. Reading, flossing and most daily chores are invisible to Health, so you log them by hand.

Is Nularo a habit tracker?

A narrow one. It does not let you create custom habits. It reads sleep, steps, active minutes, workouts and heart data from Apple Health, gives a morning verdict in one of four moods, and recalculates Creature Age weekly from those habits. It is iPhone only and costs $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month.
