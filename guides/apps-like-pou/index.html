Pou in 2026: still here, and not free on iPhone

Pou is still alive and still updated. Zakeh Limited released it on iPhone in 2012, and the latest version landed on November 24, 2026. The loop has not changed: feed it, clean it, play games in the Game Room for coins, mix potions in the Lab, and spend the coins on outfits, hats and wallpapers. There is also Pou 3D, a separate $1.99 app from the same developer.

Two things surprise people coming from Android:

  • It costs $1.99 on iPhone. On Google Play it is free to install and lists over 1 billion downloads. The US App Store version is paid, has 4.4 stars from about 3,900 ratings, and still contains ads.
  • The coins are for sale. The US listing shows coin packs from $0.99 up to $24.99, plus potions and body colors at $0.99.

Pou has no Apple Watch app and does not read Apple Health. It knows only what you do inside the game. That is fine for a kid. For an adult, it is the reason many people start looking for something else.

Games that play like Pou

My Boo is the nearest copy of the Pou loop, and its own App Store subtitle says as much. Boo is a small round pet you feed and look after daily, with adventure mini-games that earn coins for outfits for dogs, cats, rabbits and other characters, and several games that work offline. It has 4.5 stars from about 19,000 US ratings. Free with ads; removing them is $1.99, and coin packs run from $1.99 to $9.99.

My Talking Tom 2 is the big-budget version. You feed Tom, bathe him, brush his teeth, take him to the toilet, teach him tricks like drums and boxing, and fly to islands with flight tokens. It has 4.4 stars from about 859,000 US ratings. It is free, but it leans hard on ads and purchases: Remove Ads is $14.99 and coin packs go up to $99.99. The listing also shows gacha-style rewards.

Bubbu is a cat who needs to be fed, dressed, cuddled and bathed "from morning to midnight", plus a vet clinic, salon and over 30 mini-games such as 2048 and Cat Connect. 4.2 stars from about 2,600 US ratings. Free with ads; Remove Ads is $0.99, and there is a subscription at $7.49 a month or $44.99 a year.

The original: Tamagotchi

If Pou made you curious about where the idea came from, Bandai Namco has two iPhone games. My Tamagotchi Forever is the classic care loop: feed, wash, clean up after your character and turn the lights out at night, and it evolves into different characters depending on how you care for it. It is free with ads and a $4.99 Tama Club, rated 9+ because it contains loot boxes, and its last update was November 2025. Tamagotchi Adventure Kingdom is an Apple Arcade game, more adventure than care: Mametchi travels the kingdom, solves quests and builds a camp. It has 4.8 stars from about 12,000 ratings, and Apple Arcade means no ads and no in-app purchases for $6.99 a month or $49.99 a year. Grown out of feeding a blob? Pets that react to your real life

A lot of people searching for apps like Pou are not twelve anymore. They liked having a small creature to check on, and they do not want to buy coins to dress it. The grown-up version of the idea flips who does the caring: the pet does well when you look after yourself.

Nularo: a creature fed by Apple Health

Nularo is the pick if you want the daily check-in without the tapping. There is no food bowl and no Game Room. A small creature reads Apple Health read-only (sleep, sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and blood oxygen), so it reacts to the day you actually had. Nothing to log.

Each morning a verdict notification arrives and the creature lands in one of four moods: glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious. The mood is set against your own 14-day baseline, so it compares you with you. Around that sit Sleep, Stress and Energy scores, sleep debt, a Bedtime Pledge, evening Confessions, a Weekly Report Card and shareable roast cards. Creature Age, recalculated weekly once 7 days of data exist, turns your habits into an age; it is a rules-based, for-fun number, not a medical one. Friend mode shows a friend only a mood word and a good-day count, never your health numbers.

The honest limits: you cannot feed, wash or dress it, so if the care loop is what you loved, stay with My Boo or Pou. It judges rather than cheers, it works the morning after rather than in real time, and it is iPhone only. Without an Apple Watch, Apple Health has much thinner sleep and heart data to react to. It is a subscription with a paywall after onboarding: $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month.

Finch and Habitvs

Finch is the most popular adult pet on the App Store, with 4.9 stars from about 779,000 US ratings. Your bird grows and goes on adventures when you finish small self-care goals, check in on your mood or do a breathing exercise. It does not read Apple Health, so everything it knows, you told it. The core is free with an optional Finch Plus subscription; our Finch app review breaks down what is free, and Finch alternatives for adults lists pets with less tapping.

Habitvs has a creature called Kova that reacts to how you sleep, move and recover, from Apple Health, with an Apple Watch app and widgets. It is free and new enough to have no US ratings yet. We compare it with step-fed pets like Nomi and Poki in virtual pet apps that use Apple Health.

Before you hand a Pou-style game to a kid

Every free game above sells coins, and My Talking Tom 2 sells a $99.99 pack. Apple's Ask to Buy makes a child request approval before a purchase or download. On your iPhone, open Settings, tap Family, tap your child's name, tap Apps & Websites, tap Restrictions, then tap Ask to Buy and turn on Require Purchase Approval. Apple says Ask to Buy is on by default for children under 13.

If a purchase already went through by mistake, Apple lets you request a refund: sign in to reportaproblem.apple.com, choose "I'd like to", then Request a refund, pick a reason and the item, and submit.

Frequently asked questions

What is the closest app to Pou?

My Boo is the closest in look and loop: a round little pet you feed, clean and dress, with mini-games that earn coins for outfits. My Talking Tom 2 does the same with a cat and much bigger production. Both are free with ads and coin packs.

Is Pou free on iPhone?

No. On the US App Store Pou is a $1.99 purchase, and it still contains ads and sells coin packs from $0.99 to $24.99 and potions. On Google Play it is free to install, with ads and in-app purchases, and lists over 1 billion downloads.

Is there a Pou-style pet game with no ads?

Tamagotchi Adventure Kingdom is part of Apple Arcade, which Apple describes as no ads and no in-app purchases. Apple Arcade costs $6.99 a month or $49.99 a year and can be shared with up to five family members. Several free games also sell a one-off Remove Ads purchase.

Is there a virtual pet app for adults like Pou?

Yes, but adult pets usually feed on your own habits instead of taps. Finch is a free self-care bird that grows when you finish small goals. Habitvs (free) and Nularo (subscription) read Apple Health, so the pet reacts to your sleep and movement without you logging anything.

Is Nularo like Pou?

In spirit only. You do not feed or wash it. Nularo is a creature that reads your sleep, steps, workouts and heart data from Apple Health and gives a morning verdict in one of four moods. It is iPhone only and a subscription: $39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month.
