Finch alternatives for adults (2026)

Updated October 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Nularo reads your Apple Health and turns it into a creature that reacts to how you really slept and moved. Get it

Short answer

Finch is free, gentle and well loved, but you log everything yourself, and its App Store listing and privacy label mention no Apple Health access. If it feels too childish, try Habitica (a pixel RPG you can play with friends) or Daylio (a two-tap mood log). If you want less logging, Streaks ($5.99 once) completes Health-linked habits for you, How We Feel and Bearable pull sleep and activity from Apple Health, and Nularo is a pet that runs entirely on Apple Health data, with a much ruder personality.

You liked the idea of Finch. A small creature that does better when you do better is a good trick. But somewhere between the outfit shop and tapping through your bird's overnight adventure just to tick off "drink water", you started wondering if there is a version of this for grown-ups.

What Finch gets right

Credit first, because Finch earned it. It sits at 4.9 stars from roughly 765,000 ratings on the US App Store, which almost no self-care app comes close to. The core is free: Finch's own site says the daily goals, reflections, breathing tools and mood tracking cost nothing, and Finch Plus is optional. It runs on iPhone and Android, and it was updated as recently as October 2026.

The design choices are kind on purpose. You set tiny goals ("get out of bed" counts), your bird gains energy when you finish them, and missing a day does not punish you. There is a mood tracker, a gratitude prompt, a goal tracker, guided journaling, breathing exercises and quizzes. Friends can send each other "good vibes". For a lot of people, that softness is exactly why it works.

What Finch does not do is look at anything you have not told it. Its App Store description never mentions Apple Health or HealthKit, and its App Store privacy label lists no health or fitness data. So if you searched "Finch app Apple Health": as of October 2026, your bird has no idea how you slept unless you tell it.

Why people look for something else

Search the Finch subreddit for "alternatives" and you get a dozen threads. Reading through them, the reasons cluster into four themes. These are paraphrased, not quotes.

  • Too cute for some adults. One therapist who recommends Finch asked for something similar for clients who would be put off by the aesthetic. Other users say they like the app but roll their eyes at words like "birb", or want the same mechanics without cartoon animals. Plenty of commenters push back and say the cuteness is the point, which is fair.
  • Too much game. Coins, shops, seasonal themes and items you only get by keeping a streak. Several long-time users say it started to feel more like a collecting game than a self-care tool, and the fear of missing items became its own source of stress.
  • Too many taps. A recurring complaint is the number of screens between opening the app and finishing a task. Once a routine is built, some people just want a list.
  • It only knows what you type. If you already wear an Apple Watch, logging a walk your watch recorded feels like paperwork.

Each of those has a different fix, so the alternatives below are grouped by what you want instead.

Finch alternatives, by what you want instead

Same reward loop, less pastel: Habitica

Habitica turns your to-do list into a pixel-art RPG. You check off a habit, you earn gold and experience, and you collect gear and pets. It is still a game, but it reads more fantasy adventure than nursery. Its best feature for a Finch refugee is parties: you team up with friends and fight bosses by completing your own tasks, so slacking hurts the group. It is free, with an optional subscription ($4.99 a month or $47.99 a year on the US App Store). You log everything yourself.

No character at all: Daylio or Streaks

Daylio is a mood and activity diary built for speed: pick a mood, tap what you did, done. Its stats show which activities line up with better days. Free, with optional premium. It came up repeatedly on Reddit as the "less cutesy" option.

Streaks is a plain, well-made habit tracker (an Apple Design Award winner) for up to 24 daily tasks. It costs $5.99 once, with no subscription. The part that matters here: with your permission it reads the Health app and marks Health-linked tasks complete on its own, such as steps or water. It has an Apple Watch app too. Anything Health does not record, you still tick off by hand.

Mood tracking that pulls in your health data: How We Feel or Bearable

How We Feel is a free mood journal conceived with Yale's Center for Emotional Intelligence. You name what you feel from a large grid of emotions, and it uses HealthKit to track sleep and exercise alongside, so you can spot patterns. It also has a friends feature for sharing how you feel with people you trust. You still check in, but the health side fills itself.

Bearable is a highly customizable tracker for mood, energy, sleep, symptoms and habits, with charts that show what moves together. It syncs steps, sleep and heart rate from Apple Health. Free, with optional premium. It is the most data-heavy option here, which is either the point or too much.

You like cute, just not this cute: Quabble

If the problem is Finch's game layer rather than its warmth, Quabble is the closest swap. You get a duck, a little space to decorate, and a library of self-care exercises, with less emphasis on to-do lists. Free, with an optional subscription. It is still very soft, so it will not fix the "too childish" complaint.

Nularo: the opposite of Finch, on purpose

Full disclosure, this is our app. Nularo keeps one thing from Finch, a creature whose state reflects how you are treating yourself, and flips the rest.

There are no check-ins. Nularo reads Apple Health (read-only): sleep and sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and blood oxygen. Each morning you get a verdict notification, and the creature lands in one of four moods (glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious) measured against your own 14-day baseline. The roast is written fresh each morning from last night's real numbers. If you slept five hours, it already knows.

The tone is savage rather than soft. The creature judges; it does not coach, and it never hands out advice. Some people find that more motivating than a bird cheering them on. Some will hate it. (If the roast is the appeal, here are other apps that roast you, from music to Strava.) There is also a weekly Creature Age, a for-fun number built from public-health guidelines (we compare it with other apps in biological age apps for Apple Watch), and a sleep debt figure (here is what Apple Watch does and does not track on sleep debt).

If the social side of Finch is what you would miss, friend mode puts two creatures side by side. You invite someone with a 6-character code and see a this-week tally. Your friend sees your display name, today's mood word and a good-day count, never your sleep, steps or heart numbers, and never the roast. More on that in sleep tracking with a partner or friends.

The honest limits. Nularo is iPhone only. It is a subscription ($39.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, or $9.99 a month), with a hard paywall after onboarding, so there is no free tier like Finch's. It only knows what Apple Health records, so without an Apple Watch the data is thinner, and habits Health cannot see (making your bed, calling your mum) are invisible to it. And if what you want is gentle encouragement on a bad day, stay with Finch. Nularo will not give you that.

Frequently asked questions

Does Finch work with Apple Health?

Not as of October 2026, based on its App Store listing. The description never mentions Apple Health or HealthKit, and the privacy label lists no health or fitness data. Your goals, moods and reflections are things you enter by hand.

Is there an app like Finch but less childish?

Habitica keeps the reward loop (gold, gear, pets) but swaps the pastel bird for a pixel-art fantasy RPG. If you want no character at all, Daylio and Streaks are plain and fast. Nularo has a creature, but it is sarcastic rather than sweet.

Is Finch free?

Yes. Finch's own site says the core self-care features are free. Finch Plus is an optional subscription, and the App Store lists Finch Plus purchases between $5.99 and $69.99, so check the offer shown in the app before you pay.

Is there a Finch alternative where I do not have to check in every day?

Streaks can mark Health-linked habits (steps, workouts, water) done on its own. Nularo needs no check-ins at all: it reads sleep, steps, workouts and heart data from Apple Health and the creature reacts each morning. Both are iPhone apps.

Is Nularo like Finch?

It shares the core idea, a pet that reflects how you are looking after yourself, and almost nothing else. Finch encourages you and asks you to log. Nularo reads Apple Health, judges the result, and never gives advice. It is iPhone only and needs a subscription after onboarding.

Nularo is an entertainment and wellness app, not a medical device. Nothing on this page is medical advice. If something about your sleep or heart worries you, talk to a doctor.
