What Samsung's Sleep Animals actually are

Sleep Animals are part of Sleep Coaching in Samsung Health. After a short setup survey and a few nights of sleep data from a Galaxy Watch, Fit or Ring, the app gives you one of eight animals and builds a coaching program around it. Samsung's support page lists all eight: Unconcerned Lion, Sensitive Hedgehog, Nervous Penguin, Sun Averse Mole, Cautious Deer, Easygoing Walrus, Alligator on the Hunt and Exhausted Shark.

The animals are not chronotypes or personality types, even though some articles describe them that way. Samsung says each one represents a sleep pattern built from three measures: sleep duration, consistency and awake time. Three measures that can each be good or poor give exactly eight combinations, which is why there are eight animals.

Samsung has updated the requirements over time. Its 2023 materials asked for at least 7 nights including days off. A Samsung Health update announced on October 14, 2026 cut that to three days of data within the past two weeks, with a coaching program of four to eight weeks that adjusts its guidance weekly. It is rolling out through app updates, so what you see depends on your version.

Can you get one on Apple Watch or iPhone?

No, and not even with a Samsung watch. Samsung's compatibility page says Galaxy Watch4, Watch5, Watch6, Watch7, Watch Ultra and Watch FE rely on Google Play services, which are not supported on iOS. Sleep Coaching itself requires Samsung Health 6.24 or later on a Samsung Galaxy phone running Android 11 or later. If you carry an iPhone, the animals are out of reach.

Apple has no animal, no character and no multi-week coaching program. What it does have is surprisingly close underneath. Since watchOS 26, Apple Watch gives you a Sleep Score from 0 to 100 every morning, and Apple's support page says it is calculated from estimates of:

  • Sleep duration, 50 points. How long you slept.
  • Bedtime consistency, 30 points. When you fell asleep, compared with the last 13 nights.
  • Interruptions, 20 points. How often you woke up and how long you stayed awake.

Those are the same three ingredients as Samsung's animals. Apple adds them into one number and a band (Very Low up to Very High at 96 and above); Samsung turns the pattern into a cartoon. If you know which of Apple's three parts you usually lose points on, you already know roughly which animal you would be. Find your animal from your Apple Watch data

First, look at the breakdown rather than the total:

  1. On Apple Watch, open the Sleep app after you wake up and tap the Info button. Turn the Digital Crown to scroll back through the last 14 days.
  2. On iPhone, open Health, tap the Browse tab, tap Sleep, tap your Sleep Score, then tap the info icon to see how it was calculated.
  3. Tap W or M on the Sleep chart to see a week or a month. One night tells you little; Samsung also uses several nights.
  4. Tap Show More Sleep Data, then Stages, to see time and percentage Awake alongside REM, Core and Deep.

Then find your pattern. "Good" here means the part you rarely lose points on. The tiers and the descriptions for Lion, Walrus, Mole and Alligator come from BGR's 2025 explainer; Samsung's own study confirms the Penguin, Deer and Shark descriptions and says Hedgehogs and Moles are the animals with consistency problems.

On Apple's side, that translates to: losing duration points makes you short, losing bedtime consistency points makes you irregular, and losing interruption points means high awake time. A watch that keeps docking you on interruptions while the other two stay near full is a Penguin, which, for what it's worth, is the most common animal. In Samsung's 2023 global sleep study about a third of participants were Nervous Penguins.

Be honest about what this is. Samsung does not publish its cut-offs, its survey answers feed in too, and Apple does not publish how many hours earn full duration points. So this is a translation for fun, not Samsung's algorithm run on Apple data. Consistency also means different things: Samsung shows it as days this week you met your sleep goal, while Apple compares when you fell asleep against your last 13 nights. What Apple gives you instead of coaching

Samsung pairs the animal with missions, checklists, articles and meditation guidance. Apple has no program, but it does give you the tools the coaching tends to point at, all free and built in:

  • A sleep goal and schedule. Open Health, tap Browse, tap Sleep, then Full Schedule & Options to set a sleep goal, bedtime and wake-up time for each day. The slider turns orange if the schedule does not meet your goal.
  • Sleep Focus. Part of the same setup: it simplifies your Lock Screen and silences distractions at your scheduled bedtime, with exceptions for the people or apps you choose.
  • Next Wake Up Only. Changes one morning's alarm without touching the full schedule, so a one-off early start does not rewrite your routine.

None of that tells you what you should change, and this page will not either. The animals, the score and the table above describe a pattern. They are not a diagnosis. If your data looks like a Shark night after night and you feel it during the day, talk to a doctor rather than an app.

Want a creature, not a number?

Part of the appeal of a Sleep Animal is that a penguin is easier to care about than an 84. On iPhone, a few apps put a character on your sleep. Gamified sleep tracker apps covers Pokémon Sleep and SleepTown, and virtual pet apps that use Apple Health covers pets that read your data instead of asking you to log it.

Full disclosure: Nularo is our app, and it is one of those. Nularo reads Apple Health read-only (sleep and sleep stages, steps, active minutes, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and SpO2) with no logging. Each morning you get a verdict notification, and the creature lands in one of four moods: glowing, fine-but-judgy, wrecked or suspicious. The yardstick is your own 14-day baseline, so it reacts to a bad night for you, not to a fixed animal category. It also gives Sleep, Stress and Energy scores and keeps a running sleep debt.

The honest limitation: it is not a coaching program. The creature judges; it does not coach, and there are no missions or articles like Samsung's. It is iPhone only, it reports the morning after rather than in real time, and without an Apple Watch it has much thinner sleep data. Frequently asked questions

Does Apple Watch have sleep animals?

No. Sleep Animals belong to Samsung Health's Sleep Coaching, and Apple has no equivalent character or coaching program. Apple's closest feature is the Sleep Score, which scores the same three things Samsung's animals are built from: how long you slept, how consistent your bedtime is and how much you were awake.

Can I get Samsung Sleep Animals on iPhone?

No. Samsung says Galaxy Watch4 and later rely on Google Play services, which are not supported on iOS, and Sleep Coaching requires a Samsung Galaxy phone running Android 11 or later with Samsung Health 6.24 or later.

What is the most common sleep animal?

In Samsung's 2023 global sleep study, about a third of participants were Nervous Penguins, which Samsung describes as people who keep healthy circadian rhythms but are frequently interrupted during sleep. The study also found nearly 40% of people in their 70s were Cautious Deer.

What is the best sleep animal?

The Unconcerned Lion is the top tier: good sleep time, good consistency and little time awake. Samsung calls the Exhausted Shark its most substandard designation, for people who are visibly sleep deprived and yet cannot achieve quality sleep.

Is there an iPhone app with a sleep character?

Yes, a few. Pokémon Sleep and SleepTown turn sleep into a game, and Nularo gives you a creature whose mood each morning reflects your Apple Health sleep and heart data against your own 14-day baseline. Nularo is iPhone only and a subscription.
